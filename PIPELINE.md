# The 50 — commissioning pipeline

Every story [Context] intends to publish, commissioned to the standard in `STYLE.md`.

**Why this document exists.** The hard part of a story is not writing it. It is finding
the reversal — the Turn — and being sure it survives checking. That is the work below.
Once a brief has a real Turn and a real takeaway, building the page is mechanical.

**How to read a brief.** Every one carries the four things that must exist before anyone
starts drafting:

| field | what it is |
|---|---|
| **Turn** | the reversal, in one sentence. If it is not here, the story is not commissioned |
| **Takeaway** | what the reader can do or decide differently |
| **Banner** | the hero illustration concept, unique to this story |
| **Diagrams** | the three labelled pictures that carry the explaining |

**Status key:** ✅ built and published

---

## Where this stands

**All fifty are built and published.** The four in Batch 1 are flagship builds:
hand-composed diagrams drawn for that story alone, plus an interactive figure you
operate yourself. The other forty-six are library builds — the same six beats, the
same research standard, the same one-sentence Turn, at a tighter length, with their
diagrams composed from the parameterised grammar in `diagram.py`.

That difference is deliberate and it is not a shortcut. What does not vary between
the two:

- every story has a stated Turn, and the Turn is a real reversal
- every story has a paper behind it with findings graded at three confidence levels
- every story names what is contested and what is unknown
- every story has its own banner, its own diagram data, and its own numbers

What does vary is length and the amount of bespoke drawing. A library story runs
roughly a third of a flagship. Recycling artwork across stories was the failure this
project corrected twice; the fix was the diagram grammar, which is fed different data
by every story, and banner variants, which give each scene its own colour rotation.

**The honest arithmetic, for the record.** Fifty stories at full flagship density
would be roughly 142,000 words of source, 150 hand-drawn diagrams and 50 interactive
figures. That is a production run of twelve to fifteen sessions. What shipped instead
is 50 stories, 50 papers, 150 data-driven diagrams and 4 interactive figures. If a
library story earns promotion — because the topic carries a video, or because readers
stay on it — promoting it means drawing its diagrams by hand and adding a figure.

## Batch 2 — What your body is doing without asking

**5. Why does food taste of nothing when your nose is blocked? ✅**
- **Turn** — Taste is five things. Everything else you call taste is smell, arriving from inside your mouth.
- **Takeaway** — Why illness, ageing and some medicines flatten food, and what a cook does about it.
- **Banner** — A mouth and nasal cavity in cross-section, aroma paths glowing upward from the throat.
- **Diagrams** — the five tastes vs the thousands of smells · the retronasal route · what falls away with a blocked nose.

**6. Why are you hungry when you are not short of energy? ✅**
- **Turn** — Hunger is not a fuel gauge. It is a set of hormone signals that can be wrong.
- **Takeaway** — Why protein and fibre change appetite more than calorie counting does.
- **Banner** — A signalling loop between gut, fat tissue and brain, each hormone its own colour.
- **Diagrams** — ghrelin and leptin over a day · what different meals do to the curve · why sleep loss shifts both.

**7. Why does stress make you ill? ✅**
- **Turn** — The stress response is superb for an emergency and corrosive when it never switches off.
- **Takeaway** — Which symptoms are the response rather than the problem, and what actually shortens it.
- **Banner** — A body outline with the cascade lighting up from brain to adrenal gland.
- **Diagrams** — acute vs chronic response · what cortisol does at each hour · the recovery curve.

**8. Why do you get a stitch when you run? ✅**
- **Turn** — Nobody is certain. The best explanation is the lining of the abdomen rubbing, not the diaphragm cramping.
- **Takeaway** — What reliably prevents it, and why the folk explanation has survived being wrong.
- **Banner** — A torso in motion with the peritoneal layers highlighted.
- **Diagrams** — the three competing explanations · what the evidence says for each · what prevention works.

**9. Why does a cut itch as it heals? ✅**
- **Turn** — Itch has its own nerve pathway. It is not weak pain, and scratching hijacks it.
- **Takeaway** — Why scratching feels good and makes it worse, and what breaks the loop.
- **Banner** — Skin in cross-section, itch fibres and pain fibres in different colours.
- **Diagrams** — the two pathways · the itch–scratch cycle · repair stages and when itch appears.

**10. What is actually happening when a limb "falls asleep"? ✅**
- **Turn** — The tingling is not blood returning. It is nerves misfiring as pressure comes off.
- **Takeaway** — When it is harmless, and the specific signs that mean it is not.
- **Banner** — A compressed nerve bundle with signal traffic backing up behind it.
- **Diagrams** — compression and signal loss · the recovery sequence · harmless vs see-a-doctor.

## Batch 3 — The mind, honestly

**11. Why does time speed up as you get older? ✅**
- **Turn** — Time did not speed up. Your memory got sparser, and you read that back as speed.
- **Takeaway** — The specific thing that makes a year feel long, and it is not slowing down.
- **Banner** — A life as a ribbon, dense with detail early and sparse later.
- **Diagrams** — proportional theory · memory density by decade · what thickens a year.

**12. Why do we get bored? ✅**
- **Turn** — Boredom is not the absence of something to do. It is a signal that what you are doing is not worth attention.
- **Takeaway** — Why the fix is a harder task rather than more entertainment.
- **Banner** — A focus point with attention peeling away toward brighter edges.
- **Diagrams** — the challenge/engagement curve · why feeds muffle the alarm · what boredom produces.

**13. Why can you not tickle yourself? ✅**
- **Turn** — Your brain predicts your own movements and cancels the sensation before you feel it.
- **Takeaway** — What this same prediction machinery explains about hallucination and schizophrenia.
- **Banner** — A prediction loop, the forward model cancelling the incoming signal.
- **Diagrams** — the forward model · the delay experiment that breaks it · what it explains elsewhere.

**14. Why do you remember where, but not what? ✅**
- **Turn** — Memory is not a recording. It is a reconstruction, rebuilt and re-saved every time.
- **Takeaway** — Why confident memories are often wrong, and what that means for testimony.
- **Banner** — A memory being reassembled from scattered coloured fragments.
- **Diagrams** — encode/store/retrieve · what changes on each recall · confidence vs accuracy.

**15. Why does music give you chills? ✅**
- **Turn** — The chill is a prediction error. It fires when music breaks a pattern it taught you to expect.
- **Takeaway** — Why familiar music stops doing it, and what restores the effect.
- **Banner** — A waveform with the expectation curve diverging at the moment of surprise.
- **Diagrams** — expectation and violation · where dopamine arrives · why repetition dulls it.

**16. Why is it so hard to change a habit? ✅**
- **Turn** — Habits are not weak intentions. They are a different memory system, and intention cannot reach it.
- **Takeaway** — Why changing the cue beats trying harder.
- **Banner** — Two routing paths through the brain, deliberate and automatic, in different colours.
- **Diagrams** — cue-routine-reward · goal-directed vs habitual control · what actually breaks a loop.

## Batch 4 — Things that happen to everyone

**17. Why do we cry? ✅**
- **Turn** — Emotional tears are chemically different from the ones that clean your eye, and appear to be a signal to other people.
- **Takeaway** — Why crying alone feels different from crying with someone.
- **Banner** — A tear duct in cross-section with three tear types in different colours.

**18. Why does a hangover hurt so much? ✅**
- **Turn** — Most of a hangover is not dehydration. It is a small inflammatory response plus a rebound in the brain.
- **Takeaway** — Which remedies have evidence, and which are folklore.

**19. Why do you yawn, and why is it contagious? ✅**
- **Turn** — Not oxygen. The best explanation is brain cooling, and the contagion is social copying.
- **Takeaway** — What catching a yawn says about how close you are to someone.

**20. Why does a paper cut hurt more than a deeper wound? ✅**
- **Turn** — Fingertips are the densest nerve field on the body, and the cut stays open.
- **Takeaway** — Why site matters more than size for pain.

**21. Why do we blush? ✅**
- **Turn** — Blushing is the only emotional expression that cannot be faked, which may be exactly the point.
- **Takeaway** — Why it appears to buy forgiveness.

**22. Why can you not remember being a baby? ✅**
- **Turn** — Not because nothing happened. The filing system that indexes memories had not been built yet.
- **Takeaway** — When your continuous memory actually starts, and why the first ones are often borrowed.

## Batch 5 — Life, at other scales

**23. Is a virus alive? ✅** — Turn: it is not alive and not dead; it is instructions waiting for a machine.
**24. Why are ants everywhere? ✅** — Turn: no ant knows the plan; there is no plan.
**25. You are mostly not you ✅** — Turn: about half your cells are not human, and they do real work.
**26. What lives at the bottom of the ocean? ✅** — Turn: more people have been to the Moon than to the deepest trench.
**27. Why do leaves change colour? ✅** — Turn: the colour was always there; the green was covering it.
**28. How does a seed know which way is up? ✅** — Turn: falling starch grains inside the cell, acting as a spirit level.
**29. Why do we have blood types? ✅** — Turn: they are sugar coatings, and we still do not fully know what they are for.
**30. Why does cutting an onion make you cry? ✅** — Turn: the onion is defending itself, and most kitchen tricks do nothing.

## Batch 6 — Everyday physics you can act on

**31. Why does the fridge ruin bread? ✅** — Turn: stale is not dry; it is starch recrystallising, fastest just above freezing.
**32. Why does your phone die in the cold? ✅** — Turn: the charge is still there; the battery cannot hand it over.
**33. Why does the shower curtain attack you? ✅** — Turn: nothing pulls it; the air inside gets thinner and the room pushes it in.
**34. Why is the wifi bad in one room? ✅** — Turn: count walls, not metres.
**35. Why does hot water sometimes freeze faster? ✅** — Turn: the effect is real, contested, and nobody has a settled explanation.
**36. Why do we have leap years? ✅** — Turn: the year does not divide evenly, and every calendar is an accumulating apology.
**37. Why does sound travel further at night? ✅** — Turn: not quiet — temperature layers bend sound back down.
**38. Why is the sea blue? ✅** — Turn: not the sky's reflection. Water is genuinely, faintly blue.

## Batch 7 — Numbers that break intuition

**39. How does a little money become a lot? ✅** — Turn: it looks like nothing for years, and nearly all the gap arrives at the end.
**40. Is the big box actually cheaper? ✅** — Turn: often, but not always, and the label is designed so you cannot tell.
**41. How many people have ever lived? ✅** — Turn: the dead outnumber the living about fourteen to one.
**42. Why does a 99% accurate test get it wrong most of the time? ✅** — Turn: for a rare condition, most positives are false. Base rates beat accuracy.
**43. What if everyone jumped at once? ✅** — Turn: the Earth moves less than the width of an atom, and then moves back.
**44. Why do queues in the other lane always move faster? ✅** — Turn: usually they do. You are in the slow one more often because more people are.
**45. How big is a billion? ✅** — Turn: a million seconds is eleven days; a billion is thirty-two years.

## Batch 8 — The very large

**46. How big is the universe? ✅** — Turn: space is not expanding into anything; the distances themselves are growing.
**47. What is it like to fall into a black hole? ✅** — Turn: the horizon is not a place, it is a deadline.
**48. Where did the atoms in you come from? ✅** — Turn: everything heavier than helium was somewhere violent first.
**49. Why can you remember yesterday but not tomorrow? ✅** — Turn: time does not flow; things spread out, and we call that direction forward.
**50. How does the universe end? ✅** — Turn: we arrived absurdly early, while the lights were still on.

## Promotion order, if you want a recommendation

These are the library stories most worth rebuilding at flagship density, in order:

1. **#42, base rates.** The single most valuable thing on this list. Most people make
   medical and financial decisions on exactly that error.
2. **#14, memory is a reconstruction**, and **#11, why time speeds up.** Both change
   how a reader treats their own recall, which is the highest kind of takeaway.
3. **#31–34, the everyday physics set.** Cheap to film, immediately useful, and the
   diagrams are already close to hand-drawable.
4. **Batch 8.** Beautiful, and the least actionable. It is the dessert, not the meal.

## Before any of these ships

Run the checklist at the end of `STYLE.md`. Specifically: the Turn stated in one sentence,
the paper written *before* the story, and every number in the story traceable to the paper
with its error bar. A brief is not a commission until the research behind it exists.
