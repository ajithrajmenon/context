# The fifty — the catalogue

Every story [Context] has published, filed under the question it asks.

**The framework.** Every story asks exactly one of four questions. That is decided
before the title is written, and the title obeys it — see `STYLE.md` section 2. A
piece that cannot say which of the four it is asking has not decided what it is for.

**The scope.** This planet only. Nothing beyond Earth. A story about a black hole
ends in awe; a story about antibiotic resistance ends in a decision, and we are in
the second business. The five cosmology stories that used to sit here were removed
when the scope was set, not archived — they were off-brief, not unfinished.

**Two builds.** ★ marks a flagship: hand-composed diagrams drawn for that story
alone, plus an interactive figure. The rest are library builds — same six beats,
same research standard, same one-sentence Turn, at a tighter length, with diagrams
composed from the parameterised grammar in `diagram.py`. Every story of either kind
has its own banner, its own diagram data, its own numbers and its own paper.

## What — 10 stories

*What the thing actually is. Definitions, inventories, scale — the stories where the surprise is in the answer to a plain question.*

| Story | Domain | Turn |
|---|---|---|
| **What actually happens when you die?** ★ | Death | You do not die all at once. Different parts of you stop at different times, over hours. |
| **What is happening when a limb falls asleep?** | Your body | Pins and needles are not circulation returning. They are hundreds of nerve fibres coming back online at slightly different times. |
| **Is a virus alive?** | Life | A virus is not alive and not dead. It is a set of instructions waiting for a machine that can read them. |
| **You are mostly not you** | Life | You are not an individual. You are a negotiation, and most of the other party lives in your large intestine. |
| **What lives at the bottom of the ocean?** | Earth | More people have stood on the Moon than have been to the deepest point in the sea. |
| **How many people have ever lived?** | People | You are living in the most crowded moment in human history, and the safest one anyone has ever been born into. |
| **Why do you get a stitch when you run?** | Your body | It is probably not your diaphragm at all. It is two sheets of tissue inside your abdomen rubbing where they should be gliding. |
| **Can hot water freeze faster than cold?** | Physics | The effect is real enough to keep being reported and slippery enough that nobody has managed to state the conditions under which it happens. |
| **Is the big box actually cheaper?** | Money | The big box is not a discount. It is a bet that you will not do the division, and it is a bet that pays. |
| **How big is a billion?** | Numbers | You do not have an intuition for large numbers. You have an intuition for the shape of the word, and every large number is roughly the same shape. |

## Why — 23 stories

*Why it happens at all. Cause and purpose: the reason something evolved, or the reason a system behaves the way it does.*

| Story | Domain | Turn |
|---|---|---|
| **Why do we sleep?** ★ | Sleep | Sleep is not the brain switching off. It is maintenance that cannot run while you are using the machine. |
| **Why do we get old?** ★ | Ageing | Evolution has no reason to keep you in good repair after you have had children. Ageing is where it stops paying. |
| **Why does time speed up as you get older?** | Mind | You are not measuring time. You are measuring how much of it your memory bothered to keep. |
| **Why can you not tickle yourself?** | Mind | You cannot tickle yourself because your brain already knew, in detail, exactly what it was about to feel. |
| **Why are confident memories so often wrong?** | Mind | You do not remember the event. You remember the last time you remembered it. |
| **Why does music give you chills?** | Mind | The chill is not the music being beautiful. It is your prediction being wrong in a way that immediately turns out to be right. |
| **Why are ants everywhere?** | Life | No ant knows the plan. There is no plan. The colony farms, builds, wages war and adapts regardless. |
| **Why does the shower curtain attack you?** | Home | Nothing is pulling the curtain. The air inside got thinner, and the room pushed it in. |
| **Why is the wifi bad in that one room?** | Tech | Where the router sits is worth more than what the router cost. |
| **Why does a 99% accurate test get it wrong most of the time?** | Numbers | A positive result does not tell you the chance you are ill. It tells you to multiply by how likely you were before the test. |
| **Why do we cry?** | Emotion | Emotional tears are not a release valve. They are a message, and the recipient is somebody else. |
| **Why do you yawn, and why is it catching?** | Your body | A yawn appears to be a radiator, not a breath. It is your brain shedding a little heat at a moment of transition. |
| **Why does a paper cut hurt so much?** | Your body | It hurts because of where it is, not what it is. The densest sensor field on your body has been given a ragged wound that never gets to close. |
| **Why do we blush?** | Emotion | Blushing is not a malfunction. It is a confession your body makes on your behalf, and it is believed precisely because you did not choose to make it. |
| **Why can you not remember being a baby?** | Mind | The memories are not deleted. They were filed before there was anyone to file them under. |
| **Why do we get bored?** | Mind | Boredom is not emptiness. It is your attention system filing a complaint, and it is designed to be unbearable enough that you act on it. |
| **Why do leaves change colour?** | Nature | The tree is not painting the leaf. It is withdrawing the green and letting you see what was underneath the whole time. |
| **Why do we have blood types?** | Your body | Blood types are not blood varieties. They are sugar coatings, and your immune system decided its position on them before you were a year old. |
| **Why does cutting an onion make you cry?** | Kitchen | The onion is not making you cry. It is releasing a chemical weapon on being wounded, and your eye is rinsing it off. |
| **Why do we have leap years?** | Time | The rule looks arbitrary because it is not a rule about time. It is a running repayment schedule for a debt that can never be cleared exactly. |
| **Why does sound travel further at night?** | Physics | It is not the silence. The night air is acting as a lens, and it is pointed at you. |
| **Why is the sea blue?** | Physics | The sea is blue for the same reason a forest is green: it absorbs everything else. Blue is what survives the journey. |
| **Why does the other queue always move faster?** | Numbers | You are usually in the slower queue because in any set of queues, most people are. Somebody has to be, and there are more of them than there are… |

## How — 11 stories

*How it works. The machinery, step by step, in the order it runs.*

| Story | Domain | Turn |
|---|---|---|
| **The war going on inside you right now** ★ | Immunity | Every symptom you hate is your own body fighting. Winning is what feels awful. |
| **How much of taste is actually smell?** | Senses | You have almost never tasted your food. You have smelled it, from the inside, and called that taste. |
| **How does your body decide you are hungry?** | Your body | Appetite is not measuring what is in the tank. It is a prediction, and predictions can be trained. |
| **How does stress turn into illness?** | Your body | Nothing about the stress response is broken. It is a short-term system being asked to run permanently, and that is a different thing. |
| **How does an itch work?** | Your body | Scratching does not relieve the itch. It replaces it with mild pain, which the brain prefers, and the itch returns worse. |
| **How does a habit take hold?** | Mind | You cannot argue with a habit, because the part of you making the argument is not the part performing the behaviour. |
| **How does bread go stale?** | Kitchen | Chilling bread does not preserve it. It accelerates the one process that ruins it. |
| **How does cold kill a battery?** | Tech | Your phone did not lose its charge in the cold. It lost the ability to reach it, and the gauge lost the ability to describe it. |
| **How does a little money become a lot?** | Money | Starting ten years earlier usually beats doubling the amount. Time is the input that compounds; money is not. |
| **How does a hangover work?** | Your body | A hangover is mostly a small inflammatory illness, layered on a bad night's sleep and a rebound in a nervous system that spent the evening suppressed. |
| **How does a seed know which way is up?** | Nature | A seed does not know which way is up. It has grains of starch that fall, and a growth response wired to where they land. |

## What if — 6 stories

*What would happen. Counterfactuals worked honestly, to find out which parts of a system are load-bearing.*

| Story | Domain | Turn |
|---|---|---|
| **What if everyone jumped at once?** | Numbers | The planet would shift by a fraction of an atom's width, and then shift straight back. The jump is a rounding error. |
| **What if every insect disappeared?** | Life | The collapse would not come through the fruit aisle. It would come through the soil, because the things that clear the dead are the things nobody… |
| **What if the Earth stopped spinning?** | Physics | The spin is not something the Earth does to you. It is something you are doing with the Earth, which is exactly why stopping it would be… |
| **What if antibiotics stopped working?** | Health | Resistance is not a failure of the drugs. It is the predictable result of using them, which means the question was never whether it happens but… |
| **What if the internet went down for a week?** | Tech | The internet is not mostly a communication network any more. It is the coordination layer for the physical movement of goods, and that layer… |
| **What if everyone stopped eating meat?** | Food | The strongest argument for eating less meat is not what it stops putting into the air. It is what it lets grow back. |

## On the distribution

Why is the largest lens and What if the smallest, and that is deliberate rather
than a gap to fill. Most curiosity is causal, so Why is where the questions
naturally are. A counterfactual only earns its place when working it out reveals
something a direct question would not — otherwise it is a party trick. Do not pad a
lens to balance the grid.

## Promotion order, if you want a recommendation

The library stories most worth rebuilding at flagship density, in order:

1. **Why does a 99% accurate test get it wrong most of the time?** The single most
   valuable thing on this list. Most people make medical and financial decisions on
   exactly that error.
2. **Why are confident memories so often wrong?** and **Why does time speed up as
   you get older?** Both change how a reader treats their own recall.
3. **The What if set.** Newest, and the lens with the most room — a counterfactual
   worked properly is the format that carries a ten-minute video best.
4. **The everyday physics set.** Cheap to film, immediately useful, and the
   diagrams are already close to hand-drawable.

## Before anything new ships

Run the checklist at the end of `STYLE.md`. Specifically: name the lens before the
title, state the Turn in one sentence, write the paper *before* the story, and make
every number traceable to the paper with its error bar.
