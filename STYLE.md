# The [Context] Bible

The house rules. Everything we publish follows this, and anything that does not is
either wrong or a deliberate exception someone can justify out loud.

---

## 1. What we are

**[Context] is a research house that publishes in two layers.**

- The **story** is the public face. It is short, illustrated, and designed to be
  finished. Its job is to make one idea land and stay landed.
- The **paper** sits behind it. It is long, sourced, and honest about what is not
  known. Its job is to earn the story the right to be believed.

Nobody has to read the paper. Everybody has to be able to.

That split is the whole business. A story without a paper behind it is content. A
paper without a story in front of it never gets read. We do both, and we link them
in public, because being checkable is the product.

**We are not simplifiers.** Simplifying means removing until it is easy.
We do the harder thing: find the one framing under which the real thing is
already easy, and hand the reader that.

### The name

The name is written **[Context]**, brackets included, everywhere it appears —
in the wordmark, in page titles, in prose, in this document. Not *Context*, not
*context*, not *CONTEXT*.

The brackets are load-bearing. In writing, brackets are what an editor puts
around the thing they had to add so a quotation would make sense on its own.
That is the job: we are the bracketed part. The wordmark animates the two
brackets and nothing else, because the mark is the punctuation, not the word.

The only exception is a URL or a filename, where brackets are not usable.

## 2. The storytelling language

Every story is built from six named beats, in this order. They are the grammar.
When a piece feels wrong, it is almost always because one of these is missing or
out of sequence.

### The Wrong Picture
Open by stating, generously and without mockery, the thing the reader already
believes. Not a strawman — the *good* version, the one a thoughtful person holds.
If the reader does not nod here, nothing after it can work.

> Death as a moment. Ageing as wearing out. Sleep as time off.

### The Crack
One concrete observation the Wrong Picture cannot survive. Not an argument — an
observation. Preferably something the reader already knows and has never had to
reconcile.

> A donated heart restarts in someone else hours later.

### The Turn
One sentence. Set large. The reversal the whole piece exists to deliver. It is
the line someone repeats to a friend three days later, and if you cannot write
it in one sentence you do not yet have a story.

> You do not die all at once.

### The Machinery
Now, and only now, how it actually works. This is where the diagrams do the
labour. If a paragraph here is doing work a picture could do better, the
paragraph is wrong.

### The Cost
What this changes for the reader. A number they can act on, a decision it
reframes, a symptom it explains. Wonder is not enough on its own — an idea that
changes nothing is entertainment.

### The Long View
Two or three sentences that place the idea in something larger, then stop.
Never a moral. Never "and so we see that". The reader draws the conclusion; we
just get them to the vantage point.

## 3. Voice

**Register: a well-read adult explaining something they know cold to another
adult over a drink.** Not a lecture, not a children's book, not a press release.

We had a period of writing in very short, very plain sentences and it read as
juvenile. Clarity does not come from short words. It comes from correct order.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Vary sentence length. Long, then short. | Chains of six-word sentences |
| Use the precise word and gloss it once | Reach for the baby word and lose the meaning |
| Trust the reader to hold a subordinate clause | Chop every idea into its own sentence |
| State the number | "A very large amount" |
| Say what is contested, in the story, not just the paper | Present a live debate as settled |
| Concrete nouns, active verbs | Abstractions doing the acting |

**Rules that do not bend:**

- **Second person, present tense**, wherever it is honest. The reader is in it.
- **No exclamation marks.** Ever. If the fact is not remarkable on its own, the
  punctuation will not save it.
- **No rhetorical questions as headings.** A heading is a claim or a signpost.
- **Never "scientists say" or "studies show".** Say who, or say we do not know.
- **Own the uncertainty.** "The best current explanation" and "this is contested"
  are the strongest things you can write. They make everything unhedged credible.
- **No moral at the end.** Give the vantage point and get out of the way.
- **Numbers are approximate and say so.** "About", "roughly", "on the order of".
  False precision is the fastest way to lose a careful reader.

### Sentence rhythm

The unit of pace is the paragraph, not the sentence. Two to four sentences,
with at least one of them long enough to carry a clause. Then a short one to
land it. That alternation is the sound of the house.

## 4. Design language

### The split

**White page. Dark scenes. Light diagrams.** Three surfaces, three jobs, never
mixed up:

| Surface | Used for | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| White page | reading | body text lives here, nowhere else |
| Dark scene panel | atmosphere, chapter breaks | may move; carries no information |
| Light diagram card | explaining | never moves; carries all the information |

The reference work in this genre lives in the dark. We live in daylight, and the
dark is where the scenes happen. That contrast is the identity — do not soften it
by tinting the page or lightening the panels.

### Motion

**Only the hero panel moves.** Anything a reader is decoding holds still. A
diagram that drifts while you are reading it is a design failure, not a feature.

Interaction is different from animation: one operable figure per story is
correct, because the reader drives it.

Everything obeys `prefers-reduced-motion`, and reduced motion gets a composed
static frame — never a blank one.

### Colour

Two palettes, deliberately different, never interchanged.

**Scene palette** — light, saturated, sits on the deep navy ground. Cyan, lime,
magenta, violet, amber, emerald, coral, sky, orchid, teal. Scenes carry many
hues at once; a single-hue panel reads as decoration.

**Diagram palette** — darker, for ink-on-paper legibility. Every diagram colour
must clear 4.5:1 against the light card when used for text, 3:1 when it carries
meaning as a fill.

**The scene ground is one constant deep navy across the whole site.** Tinting it
per story makes the background compete with the artwork, and the background
always wins. The illustration supplies the colour.

### Diagrams

A diagram earns its place by carrying something the prose would carry worse.

- Every part that matters is **labelled**. Unlabelled shapes are decoration.
- Leader lines run in the same vertical order as the labels they point to.
  Crossed leaders read as a mistake because they are one.
- One idea per diagram. Two ideas means two diagrams.
- The caption underneath says what to *notice*, not what the diagram *is*.
- Never repeat the caption inside the diagram.
- Colour is a key, not a mood: the same colour means the same thing throughout
  a story.

### Type

Gabarito for display, Source Sans 3 for body, both self-hosted. No font CDN —
no third-party request on any page we serve.

Measure stays near 58 characters. Headings get `text-wrap: balance`.

### Cards

Rounded, generous, one idea each. A card is the unit of thought: if a reader
stops in the middle of one, it was doing two things.

## 5. Research standard

The story is downstream of the paper. Never write the story first and source it
afterwards — that produces confident prose around a hollow middle, and it shows.

**Before publishing, every story must satisfy:**

1. Every number in the story appears in the paper with its origin and its error
   bar.
2. Every claim is one of: well established, current best explanation, or
   contested — and the paper says which.
3. Where a famous version of the fact is wrong, the paper says so explicitly and
   the story mentions it.
4. Nothing is stated more confidently in the story than in the paper.
5. Anything we could not establish is written down as an open question rather
   than smoothed over.

**The paper is public and linked from the end of every story.** Not a footnote —
a proper invitation, in its own card, phrased as an offer rather than a
disclaimer.

If a finding turns out to be wrong later, the paper gets a dated correction at
the top and the story gets amended. Corrections are published, never quietly
edited. Accuracy is the product; a wrong explanation costs more trust than a
right one earns.

## 6. Structure of a story page

```
hero            dark, animated, the Wrong Picture stated in the standfirst
text            the Wrong Picture, generously
diagram         labelled, the setup
text            the Crack
turn            full colour, one sentence
diagram         the Machinery
text            the mechanism, in prose
figure          the one thing the reader operates
steps           what it means in practice, where the topic allows
diagram         the Cost, quantified
zoomout         the Long View
paper link      the offer
```

Not every story needs every block. Every story needs the six beats.

---

## 7. Structure of a paper

```
title, subtitle, date, reading time
abstract          what we set out to establish, and what we found
context           why the question matters and who has asked it before
findings          numbered, each with evidence and confidence level
what is contested honest account of the live disagreements
what we could not establish
method            how we did the work and what we did not do
further reading   real sources, described so a reader knows what each is for
```

Papers are written to be skimmed by headings and read by paragraph. Three to
five pages. Longer means it should have been two papers.

---

## 8. Checklist before anything ships

- [ ] Can you state the Turn in one sentence?
- [ ] Does the Wrong Picture describe what a thoughtful person actually believes?
- [ ] Is the Crack an observation, not an argument?
- [ ] Is every diagram labelled, still, and doing work prose could not?
- [ ] Does anything move that the reader is trying to read?
- [ ] Is there a number the reader can act on?
- [ ] Does the paper exist, and is it linked?
- [ ] Is every figure in the story traceable to the paper?
- [ ] Have you removed the moral at the end?
- [ ] Read it aloud. Does it sound like an adult or a worksheet?
