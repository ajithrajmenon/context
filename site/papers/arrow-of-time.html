Why can you remember yesterday but not tomorrow: the research

Thermodynamic asymmetry and the past hypothesis.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the origin of temporal asymmetry given time-symmetric fundamental laws. The statistical account is standard but requires a low-entropy boundary condition that remains unexplained.

What we looked at

We examined the origin of temporal asymmetry given time-symmetric fundamental laws. The statistical account is standard but requires a low-entropy boundary condition that remains unexplained.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Fundamental physical laws are very nearly time-symmetric.

    Well established

    With a small exception in weak interactions.

  2. 2

    Entropy increase is statistical, not a fundamental prohibition.

    Well established

    Standard statistical mechanics.

  3. 3

    The early universe was in a very low entropy state.

    Well established

    Required by observation.

  4. 4

    Memory formation is thermodynamically irreversible.

    Well established

    Recording requires dissipating energy.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Why the initial entropy was low.

Proposals include inflation, multiverse selection, and treating it as a brute fact. None is accepted, and some argue the question is malformed.

What we could not establish

  • Whether the arrow of time is fundamental or emergent.
  • How the quantum measurement arrow relates to the thermodynamic one.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of statistical mechanics and philosophy of physics literature. We have distinguished what is derived from what is assumed as a boundary condition.

Further reading

    A book-length treatment of exactly this gap.
