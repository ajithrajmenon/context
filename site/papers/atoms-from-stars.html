Where did the atoms in you come from: the research

Nucleosynthesis: production sites of the elements and observational evidence.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We reviewed the origin of the elements across Big Bang, stellar and compact-object nucleosynthesis, and the observational evidence for each pathway.

What we looked at

We reviewed the origin of the elements across Big Bang, stellar and compact-object nucleosynthesis, and the observational evidence for each pathway.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Big Bang nucleosynthesis produced hydrogen, helium and trace lithium only.

    Well established

    Predicted abundances match observation closely.

  2. 2

    Carbon, nitrogen and oxygen are produced by stellar fusion.

    Well established

    Well modelled and observationally supported.

  3. 3

    Fusion releases energy only up to iron.

    Well established

    Direct from nuclear binding energies.

  4. 4

    Neutron star mergers produce heavy r-process elements.

    Well established

    Directly observed in GW170817 in 2017.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

The relative contribution of neutron star mergers versus rare supernovae to r-process elements.

GW170817 confirmed mergers contribute; whether they dominate is an active question.

What we could not establish

  • Why the observed lithium abundance is lower than predicted.
  • How much of the early enrichment came from the first generation of stars.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of nuclear astrophysics references. Abundance claims are tied to spectroscopic measurement.
