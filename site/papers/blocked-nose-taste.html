Why does food taste of nothing with a cold: the research

How much of flavour perception is olfactory, and what happens when that channel closes.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We set out to quantify the split between gustation and olfaction in what people call taste. The honest answer is that the tongue contributes far less than intuition suggests, and that retronasal olfaction carries most of the identifiable character of food.

What we looked at

We set out to quantify the split between gustation and olfaction in what people call taste. The honest answer is that the tongue contributes far less than intuition suggests, and that retronasal olfaction carries most of the identifiable character of food.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    The tongue detects five basic qualities.

    Well established

    A sixth, for fat, has support but is not settled.

  2. 2

    Most flavour identification is retronasal olfaction.

    Well established

    Demonstrated trivially by nose-clip experiments.

  3. 3

    Smell declines earlier and faster than taste with age and illness.

    Well established

    Consistent across clinical measures.

  4. 4

    Loss of smell is an early marker in some neurodegenerative disease.

    Best current explanation

    Association is robust; usefulness as a screening tool is not.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Is there a distinct fat taste?

Receptor evidence is good and perceptual evidence is mixed. Whether it qualifies as basic depends on a definition nobody agrees on.

What we could not establish

  • Why some people recover smell after viral loss and others do not.
  • How much of reported taste loss in illness is genuinely gustatory.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of sensory science reviews. We relied on nose-clip and retronasal experiments rather than self-report, because self-report is exactly the thing in question.

Further reading

Described so you know what each one is for, rather than listed to look thorough.

  1. Shepherd, Neurogastronomy (2012)

    The clearest book-length case that flavour is a brain construction rather than a mouth event.

  2. Rozin (1982), on the duality of smell

    The original framing of orthonasal and retronasal as functionally different senses.
