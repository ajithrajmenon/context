Why do we have blood types: the research

ABO blood group antigens: molecular basis, distribution and proposed selective pressures.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We reviewed the biochemistry of ABO antigens, the origin of naturally occurring antibodies, and the evidence for disease-related selection. The molecular basis is settled; the evolutionary explanation is not.

What we looked at

We reviewed the biochemistry of ABO antigens, the origin of naturally occurring antibodies, and the evidence for disease-related selection. The molecular basis is settled; the evolutionary explanation is not.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    ABO types are determined by glycosyltransferase variants adding sugars to a common precursor.

    Well established

    Fully characterised.

  2. 2

    Anti-A and anti-B antibodies develop in infancy without transfusion exposure.

    Well established

    Consistently observed.

  3. 3

    Type O confers partial protection against severe malaria.

    Best current explanation

    Repeatedly found; effect size varies.

  4. 4

    Blood type diets affect health outcomes.

    Contested

    Tested directly and not supported.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Why ABO polymorphism is maintained.

Disease-mediated balancing selection is the leading account, but no single pathogen explains the global distribution.

What we could not establish

  • What, if anything, the antigens do for the cell itself.
  • Why regional frequencies differ as sharply as they do.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of transfusion medicine and population genetics literature. Claims about diet were assessed against the controlled trial that tested them.
