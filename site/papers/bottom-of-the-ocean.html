What lives at the bottom of the ocean: the research

Ocean zonation, deep-sea biology and the significance of chemosynthetic communities.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined what is established about deep-ocean life and how much of the sea floor has actually been surveyed. The zonation and the chemosynthesis findings are solid; the survey coverage is worse than most people assume.

What we looked at

We examined what is established about deep-ocean life and how much of the sea floor has actually been surveyed. The zonation and the chemosynthesis findings are solid; the survey coverage is worse than most people assume.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Photosynthesis is impossible below roughly 200 m.

    Well established

    Light attenuation is straightforward physics.

  2. 2

    Bioluminescence is common in the deep pelagic.

    Well established

    Estimates above 70% of observed animals.

  3. 3

    Chemosynthetic communities exist independent of sunlight.

    Well established

    Since the 1977 Galapagos Rift discovery.

  4. 4

    Diel vertical migration is the largest daily animal movement on Earth.

    Well established

    Visible on sonar worldwide.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How much of the deep sea has been meaningfully surveyed?

Figures around 5% for high-resolution mapping circulate widely but depend heavily on what counts as surveyed.

What we could not establish

  • How many species the deep sea holds.
  • Whether hadal trench communities are isolated from each other.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of oceanography and deep-sea biology reviews. Survey-coverage claims were treated cautiously because the definitions vary.
