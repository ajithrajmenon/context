Method, and its limits

No writing method was applied beyond transcription, because there was no verified content to write from. The research plan for this topic named real-sounding sources — a 1982 paper on asymmetric dominance, a subscription-pricing experiment, later meta-analyses and failed replications — but those appeared only in the plan's own rationale, before fact-checking, and were not present in the graded findings handed to the writer. The bias risk this avoids is the most common failure mode in this pipeline: treating a promising plan as if it were an approved finding and writing confident prose around it. The correct next step is to send this topic back through fact-checking and re-run the writing step once real findings, claims, and sources come back graded.
