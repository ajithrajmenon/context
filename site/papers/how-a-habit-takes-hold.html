How does a habit take hold: the research

Dual-process control of behaviour, and why cue-based interventions outperform intention.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined why intention predicts behaviour change so weakly. The dual-system account explains it: habitual control is cue-driven and insensitive to stated goals, and shifts toward habitual control under load.

What we looked at

We examined why intention predicts behaviour change so weakly. The dual-system account explains it: habitual control is cue-driven and insensitive to stated goals, and shifts toward habitual control under load.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Habitual and goal-directed control are dissociable systems.

    Well established

    Strong animal evidence, supported in humans.

  2. 2

    Habitual control dominates under stress, fatigue and cognitive load.

    Well established

    Directly manipulable in the laboratory.

  3. 3

    Context change disrupts habits more than intention does.

    Well established

    Field studies at relocation and life transitions.

  4. 4

    Habit formation takes a fixed number of days.

    Contested

    The popular figures are misreadings; observed range is very wide.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How much everyday behaviour is genuinely habitual?

Estimates vary widely with measurement method, and self-report is unreliable for exactly the behaviours in question.

What we could not establish

  • Whether habits are unlearned or merely overwritten.
  • Why some behaviours automate quickly and others never do.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of learning theory and behaviour change research. Field studies at natural context changes were weighted above laboratory analogues.

Further reading

Described so you know what each one is for, rather than listed to look thorough.

    The best review of habit as a distinct system.

  2. Lally et al. (2010)

    Where the widely misquoted formation-time figures actually came from, and how wide the range was.
