How big is a billion: the research

Numerical magnitude representation and public understanding of large quantities.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined evidence on how people represent large numbers and which comparisons improve accuracy. Logarithmic compression is well documented and time-based analogies measurably improve estimation.

What we looked at

We examined evidence on how people represent large numbers and which comparisons improve accuracy. Logarithmic compression is well documented and time-based analogies measurably improve estimation.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Untrained numerical estimation is approximately logarithmic.

    Well established

    Number-line studies across ages and cultures.

  2. 2

    Adults systematically underestimate the ratio between million and billion.

    Well established

    Consistent survey finding.

  3. 3

    Concrete analogies improve magnitude estimation.

    Well established

    Shown experimentally.

  4. 4

    Formal education partially but incompletely linearises estimation.

    Well established

    Developmental studies agree.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How durable analogy-based improvements are.

Immediate gains are clear; retention past a few weeks is not well studied.

What we could not establish

  • Whether any training produces a genuine intuition rather than a procedure.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of numerical cognition research. All arithmetic here is worked directly.
