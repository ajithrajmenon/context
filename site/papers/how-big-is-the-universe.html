How big is the universe: the research

Metric expansion, the particle horizon, and the observable universe.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We set out the distinction between recession due to metric expansion and motion through space, and the resulting size of the observable universe. All figures are from standard cosmological parameters.

What we looked at

We set out the distinction between recession due to metric expansion and motion through space, and the resulting size of the observable universe. All figures are from standard cosmological parameters.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Cosmological redshift results from metric expansion, not motion through space.

    Well established

    Core general relativity, confirmed observationally.

  2. 2

    The comoving radius of the observable universe is about 46.5 billion light years.

    Well established

    Follows from measured parameters.

  3. 3

    Expansion is accelerating.

    Well established

    Supernova, CMB and large-scale structure evidence agree.

  4. 4

    Distant galaxies will eventually leave our horizon permanently.

    Best current explanation

    Follows if dark energy behaves as a constant.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

What dark energy is.

It is measured well and explained badly. The cosmological constant fits the data and has no accepted physical account.

What we could not establish

  • Whether the universe is finite or infinite in extent.
  • Whether the expansion rate discrepancy between methods indicates new physics.

Method, and its limits

Standard cosmology, using published parameter values. We separated what is measured from what is inferred and from what is unmeasurable in principle.
