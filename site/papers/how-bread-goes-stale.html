How does bread go stale: the research

Starch retrogradation, its temperature dependence, and the practical consequences for bread storage.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined whether staling is moisture loss. It is not; it is starch retrogradation, which is temperature dependent with a maximum rate near refrigeration temperature. This makes the fridge the worst common storage choice.

What we looked at

We examined whether staling is moisture loss. It is not; it is starch retrogradation, which is temperature dependent with a maximum rate near refrigeration temperature. This makes the fridge the worst common storage choice.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Staling is starch retrogradation, not moisture loss.

    Well established

    Sealed-bag experiments settle it.

  2. 2

    Retrogradation is fastest just above freezing.

    Well established

    Well characterised in food science.

  3. 3

    Freezing largely arrests staling.

    Well established

    Standard commercial practice.

  4. 4

    Reheating temporarily reverses firming.

    Well established

    The crystals melt and re-form.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How much crust quality versus crumb staling should drive storage advice.

The two point in different directions: paper protects crust, plastic protects crumb. Advice depends which you care about.

What we could not establish

  • Why some breads stale far slower than others at equal water content.
