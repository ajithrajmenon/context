How many people have ever lived: the research

Estimating cumulative human births, and the long-run child mortality record.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the widely cited 117 billion figure and the historical child mortality data behind the claim that modern life is exceptional. Both hold up, with the caveat that pre-modern population estimates carry large uncertainty.

What we looked at

We examined the widely cited 117 billion figure and the historical child mortality data behind the claim that modern life is exceptional. Both hold up, with the caveat that pre-modern population estimates carry large uncertainty.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Roughly 117 billion people have ever been born.

    Best current explanation

    Population Reference Bureau estimate; wide error bars before 1500.

  2. 2

    Historical child mortality was close to 50% before 1800.

    Well established

    Consistent across many reconstructions.

  3. 3

    Global child mortality is now under 5%.

    Well established

    Well measured.

  4. 4

    Global population growth is decelerating.

    Well established

    Fertility decline is near-universal.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How reliable pre-agricultural population estimates are.

They are model outputs, not measurements, and small assumption changes move the cumulative total by billions.

What we could not establish

  • Where population will peak, and when.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of demographic estimates. We report the cumulative figure as an estimate with wide bounds rather than a measurement.
