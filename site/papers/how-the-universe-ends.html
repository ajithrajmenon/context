How does the universe end: the research

Long-term cosmological evolution and the observational window.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We reviewed projections for the far future under standard cosmology and the loss of cosmological information as expansion proceeds.

What we looked at

We reviewed projections for the far future under standard cosmology and the loss of cosmological information as expansion proceeds.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    The cosmic star formation rate peaked roughly ten billion years ago.

    Well established

    Measured across redshift.

  2. 2

    Under a cosmological constant, heat death is the expected outcome.

    Best current explanation

    Follows from current parameters.

  3. 3

    A Big Crunch is disfavoured by present measurements.

    Well established

    Expansion is accelerating.

  4. 4

    Distant galaxies will become unobservable as expansion continues.

    Best current explanation

    Follows if dark energy remains constant.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Whether dark energy is truly constant.

Some recent survey results hint it may vary with time. If it does, the far-future picture changes substantially.

What we could not establish

  • Whether proton decay occurs, which sets the timing of the late eras.
  • Whether our vacuum state is stable.

Method, and its limits

Projection from standard cosmological parameters. Every timescale here is conditional on dark energy behaving as a constant, and we have said so rather than presenting it as settled.
