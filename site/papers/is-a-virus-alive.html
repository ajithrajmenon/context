Is a virus alive: the research

Whether viruses meet standard criteria for life, and why the question persists.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined whether the alive-or-not question about viruses has an answer. It does not, and the reason is that the criteria for life were assembled for cellular organisms and were never intended to adjudicate edge cases.

What we looked at

We examined whether the alive-or-not question about viruses has an answer. It does not, and the reason is that the criteria for life were assembled for cellular organisms and were never intended to adjudicate edge cases.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Viruses have no independent metabolism.

    Well established

    Uncontroversial.

  2. 2

    Viruses evolve by natural selection.

    Well established

    The basis of vaccine updating.

  3. 3

    Roughly 8% of the human genome is of retroviral origin.

    Well established

    Well characterised by sequencing.

  4. 4

    A retroviral gene is essential to placental development.

    Best current explanation

    Syncytin; strong evidence, active area.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Should giant viruses change the definition?

Mimivirus and relatives have genomes larger than some bacteria and blur the line further. Whether that argues for redefinition or against definitions is exactly the dispute.

What we could not establish

  • Whether viruses descend from cells or predate them.
  • How much of host evolution has been driven by viral insertion.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of virology reference material. We treated the definitional question as a question about language, and reported the biology separately.
