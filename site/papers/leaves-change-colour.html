Why do leaves change colour: the research

Leaf senescence: pigment chemistry and the function of autumn anthocyanin.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the chemistry of autumn colour and the competing explanations for anthocyanin synthesis in senescing leaves. Carotenoid unmasking is settled. Anthocyanin function is not.

What we looked at

We examined the chemistry of autumn colour and the competing explanations for anthocyanin synthesis in senescing leaves. Carotenoid unmasking is settled. Anthocyanin function is not.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Autumn yellow results from chlorophyll degradation revealing existing carotenoids.

    Well established

    Straightforward pigment chemistry.

  2. 2

    Anthocyanins are newly synthesised during senescence.

    Well established

    Directly measured.

  3. 3

    Nitrogen is resorbed from leaves before abscission.

    Well established

    Central to the tree's economics.

  4. 4

    Anthocyanin acts as photoprotection during resorption.

    Best current explanation

    Strong but not exclusive support.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Photoprotection or coevolutionary signalling?

Hamilton's signalling hypothesis predicts brighter autumn colour in trees with more insect pressure, and some data fits. The photoprotection account explains more cases. Both may operate.

What we could not establish

  • Why colour intensity varies so much between years.
  • Whether warming autumns will change resorption efficiency.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of plant physiology reviews. On anthocyanin function we present both hypotheses rather than choosing, because the field has not.
