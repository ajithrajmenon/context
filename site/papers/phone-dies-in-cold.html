Why does your phone die in the cold: the research

Lithium-ion performance at low temperature, and the mechanism of cold-charging damage.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined why cold reduces apparent battery capacity and whether the popular advice is sound. Reduced ion mobility explains the capacity loss and the state-of-charge estimation error. Cold charging causes genuine, permanent damage and is the one thing worth acting on.

What we looked at

We examined why cold reduces apparent battery capacity and whether the popular advice is sound. Reduced ion mobility explains the capacity loss and the state-of-charge estimation error. Cold charging causes genuine, permanent damage and is the one thing worth acting on.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Low temperature reduces deliverable capacity, not stored charge.

    Well established

    Standard electrochemistry.

  2. 2

    State-of-charge estimation degrades in the cold.

    Well established

    Estimators are calibrated near room temperature.

  3. 3

    Charging below 0C causes lithium plating.

    Well established

    Well documented; permanent and cumulative.

  4. 4

    Capacity largely returns on warming.

    Well established

    Provided no plating occurred.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How much cumulative damage typical winter use causes.

Depends heavily on whether charging occurred while cold, which most usage data does not record.

What we could not establish

  • Real-world distribution of cold-charging events in ordinary use.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of battery engineering literature and manufacturer specifications.
