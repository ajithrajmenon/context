Why does the other queue always move faster: the research

Queue configuration, variance, and the psychology of waiting.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined why parallel queues feel slower and why serpentine queues are adopted despite identical throughput. The effect is driven by variance and by perceived fairness rather than by mean waiting time.

What we looked at

We examined why parallel queues feel slower and why serpentine queues are adopted despite identical throughput. The effect is driven by variance and by perceived fairness rather than by mean waiting time.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    With n parallel queues, the probability yours is fastest is about 1/n.

    Well established

    Direct probability.

  2. 2

    Serpentine queues reduce waiting time variance without changing the mean.

    Well established

    Standard queueing theory.

  3. 3

    Perceived fairness strongly affects reported satisfaction with a wait.

    Well established

    Replicated in service research.

  4. 4

    Occupied waits are perceived as shorter than unoccupied ones.

    Well established

    Consistently found.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Whether lane switching ever pays.

Models generally say no once switching cost is included, but real queues are not memoryless and the answer depends on the failure mode.

What we could not establish

  • How much of reported queue frustration is fairness versus duration.

Method, and its limits

Standard queueing results, combined with service psychology research.
