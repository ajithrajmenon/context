Why does the shower curtain attack you: the research

The mechanism behind shower-curtain deflection, and which explanation survives testing.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the competing explanations for curtain deflection. Buoyancy contributes but cannot be the primary cause, since the effect persists with cold water. The dominant mechanism is reduced pressure from air entrained by falling droplets.

What we looked at

We examined the competing explanations for curtain deflection. Buoyancy contributes but cannot be the primary cause, since the effect persists with cold water. The dominant mechanism is reduced pressure from air entrained by falling droplets.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    The effect occurs with cold water.

    Well established

    Easily reproduced; rules out buoyancy as sole cause.

  2. 2

    Falling droplets entrain air and drive circulation.

    Well established

    Modelled and observed.

  3. 3

    The pressure difference involved is very small.

    Well established

    Fractions of a percent of atmospheric.

  4. 4

    Weighting the hem is the most effective remedy.

    Best current explanation

    Consistent with the mechanism; no formal trials exist.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How much buoyancy contributes in a hot shower.

Both mechanisms operate together and their relative contribution has not been cleanly partitioned.

What we could not establish

  • Whether vortex formation matters as much as one widely cited simulation suggested.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of fluid dynamics discussion and the small published literature. The cold-water observation was treated as decisive between hypotheses.
