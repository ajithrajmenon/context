How does a little money become a lot: the research

Compound growth, the effect of time versus contribution, and the cost of fees.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the relative contribution of time, contribution size and fees to long-run outcomes. Time dominates by a wide margin, and fee drag is substantially larger than most savers assume because it compounds too.

What we looked at

We examined the relative contribution of time, contribution size and fees to long-run outcomes. Time dominates by a wide margin, and fee drag is substantially larger than most savers assume because it compounds too.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Compound growth is superlinear in time.

    Well established

    Arithmetic.

  2. 2

    Starting earlier typically outweighs contributing more.

    Well established

    Follows directly, for realistic parameters.

  3. 3

    Fee differences of one percent compound to large differences.

    Well established

    Well documented in fund performance analysis.

  4. 4

    Consistent market timing improves returns.

    Contested

    Evidence overwhelmingly negative for individual investors.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

What long-run real return to assume.

Historical equity returns vary by market and period; any single figure used for projection is a choice, not a fact.

What we could not establish

  • Whether historical return ranges will hold over the next several decades.

Method, and its limits

Worked arithmetically. We deliberately avoided naming a specific expected return in the story, since the shape of the curve is the point and the level is contested.
