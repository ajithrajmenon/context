Why does sound travel further at night: the research

Atmospheric refraction of sound under nocturnal temperature inversions.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the contributions of reduced background noise and atmospheric refraction to increased night-time sound propagation. Refraction under surface inversions is the dominant mechanism.

What we looked at

We examined the contributions of reduced background noise and atmospheric refraction to increased night-time sound propagation. Refraction under surface inversions is the dominant mechanism.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Sound speed in air increases with temperature.

    Well established

    Basic acoustics.

  2. 2

    Nocturnal radiative cooling produces surface temperature inversions.

    Well established

    Standard meteorology.

  3. 3

    Inversions refract sound back toward the ground, extending range.

    Well established

    Well modelled and measured.

  4. 4

    Reduced ambient noise contributes but is not the main effect.

    Best current explanation

    Consistent with the size of reported effects.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How much of the perceived effect is psychological.

Attention and expectation at night are hard to control for in field reports.

What we could not establish

  • How wind shear interacts with thermal refraction in real terrain.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of atmospheric acoustics references. Ray-bending behaviour follows directly from the sound speed gradient.
