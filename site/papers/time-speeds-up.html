Why does time speed up as you get older: the research

Why subjective duration compresses with age, and what modulates it.

August 2026 · about 6 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the two standard explanations for time compression with age. Both contribute; the memory-density account has better experimental support than the proportional account, which is largely a description rather than a mechanism.

What we looked at

We examined the two standard explanations for time compression with age. Both contribute; the memory-density account has better experimental support than the proportional account, which is largely a description rather than a mechanism.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Recalled duration tracks memory density, not clock time.

    Well established

    Robust in retrospective timing experiments.

  2. 2

    Novel periods are recalled as longer.

    Well established

    Consistent across laboratory and diary studies.

  3. 3

    The proportional account is descriptive rather than mechanistic.

    Best current explanation

    Fits the curve; explains no process.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Does perceived time also compress in the moment, or only in recall?

Prospective and retrospective timing dissociate, and most everyday reports are retrospective.

What we could not establish

  • Whether deliberate novelty-seeking durably changes recalled duration.
  • Why the effect appears to plateau in later life.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of timing research. We separated prospective from retrospective timing throughout, because conflating them produces most of the confusion in popular accounts.

Further reading

Described so you know what each one is for, rather than listed to look thorough.

  1. Wittmann, Felt Time (2016)

    The best general treatment, careful about the two kinds of timing.

  2. Zauberman et al., on memory and duration judgement

    Where the density account is tested directly.
