Is the big box actually cheaper: the research

Non-linear unit pricing in retail and consumer response to unit price information.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We reviewed evidence on quantity surcharges — cases where larger packs carry a higher unit price — and on whether unit price labelling changes behaviour.

What we looked at

We reviewed evidence on quantity surcharges — cases where larger packs carry a higher unit price — and on whether unit price labelling changes behaviour.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Quantity surcharges occur on a substantial minority of supermarket products.

    Well established

    Repeatedly found in shelf audits.

  2. 2

    Most shoppers assume larger packs are cheaper per unit.

    Well established

    Consistent survey finding.

  3. 3

    Clear unit price labelling shifts purchasing toward cheaper options.

    Well established

    Shown in field experiments.

  4. 4

    Inconsistent units on labels reduce the benefit of unit pricing.

    Best current explanation

    Strong supporting evidence.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Whether surcharges are deliberate.

Retailers cite handling and demand effects. The pattern is consistent with intentional pricing, but intent is not demonstrable from shelf data.

What we could not establish

  • How much household spending is affected in aggregate.
  • Whether online shelf displays make the problem better or worse.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of consumer economics research. Figures are ranges from published audits, not from our own sampling.
