What if everyone stopped eating meat: the research

Land use, emissions and the counterfactual of a global shift away from animal agriculture.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We reviewed land use and greenhouse gas data for animal agriculture, and modelled estimates of dietary transition. Land release and subsequent carbon uptake are less discussed than direct emissions and appear comparably large.

What we looked at

We reviewed land use and greenhouse gas data for animal agriculture, and modelled estimates of dietary transition. Land release and subsequent carbon uptake are less discussed than direct emissions and appear comparably large.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Livestock uses about 77% of agricultural land while supplying about 18% of calories.

    Well established

    Consistent across global food system analyses.

  2. 2

    Beef and lamb have emissions per unit protein far above other sources.

    Well established

    Robust to methodology, though ranges are wide.

  3. 3

    Transport is a small share of food emissions for most products.

    Well established

    Repeatedly measured.

  4. 4

    Vegetation regrowth on released land would produce large carbon uptake.

    Best current explanation

    Modelled; magnitude depends on regrowth assumptions.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Whether well-managed grazing can be net carbon negative.

Claimed for some systems and disputed; measured sequestration is generally smaller than the emissions of the herd.

What we could not establish

  • How much released land would actually revert rather than be repurposed.
  • Nutritional outcomes of a global transition in low-income settings.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of food system research. Where figures come from global averages hiding large regional spread, we say so instead of quoting a single number.
