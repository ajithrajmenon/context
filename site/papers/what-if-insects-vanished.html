What if every insect disappeared: the research

Insect decline: measured trends, ecosystem function and the consequences of loss.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined what insect loss would remove, and what the monitoring data actually shows. Decomposition and food-web support are less discussed than pollination and harder to replace. Decline is well evidenced regionally; the global rate is uncertain.

What we looked at

We examined what insect loss would remove, and what the monitoring data actually shows. Decomposition and food-web support are less discussed than pollination and harder to replace. Decline is well evidenced regionally; the global rate is uncertain.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Around 75% of leading global food crops benefit from animal pollination.

    Well established

    Widely replicated agricultural analysis.

  2. 2

    Insects are the principal agents of terrestrial decomposition.

    Well established

    Basic ecology.

  3. 3

    Substantial insect biomass declines are documented in monitored regions.

    Best current explanation

    Strong regionally; global extrapolation is disputed.

  4. 4

    Habitat loss and intensive agriculture are the leading drivers.

    Well established

    Consistent across studies.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How fast insects are declining globally.

The 2017 German biomass study reported ~75% over 27 years and was extrapolated far beyond its sampling. Later syntheses find declines that are real but smaller and highly variable by group and region.

What we could not establish

  • Whether declines have passed any functional threshold.
  • How much of the trend reverses when land use changes.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of entomology and agricultural ecology reviews. Where a headline figure came from a single region, we say so rather than reporting it as global.
