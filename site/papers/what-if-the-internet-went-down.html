What if the internet went down for a week: the research

Infrastructure interdependency and the consequences of extended network outage.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined which systems have offline fallbacks and which do not, drawing on documented regional outages and just-in-time logistics research. Payment, logistics and clinical systems are the least redundant.

What we looked at

We examined which systems have offline fallbacks and which do not, drawing on documented regional outages and just-in-time logistics research. Payment, logistics and clinical systems are the least redundant.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Card payment authorisation requires network connectivity.

    Well established

    Offline modes exist but are limited and rarely enabled.

  2. 2

    Retail supply chains hold days, not weeks, of stock.

    Well established

    Standard just-in-time practice.

  3. 3

    Voice and broadcast infrastructure is largely independent of the internet.

    Well established

    Separate networks, though convergence is increasing.

  4. 4

    Recovery time for extended outages exceeds outage duration.

    Best current explanation

    Consistent with observed regional outages.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How long a genuinely global outage could last.

No mechanism for a simultaneous global failure is well established; regional outages are the only evidence base and may not scale.

What we could not establish

  • How much offline fallback capacity still exists in payments.
  • Whether stockpiling mandates meaningfully change outcomes.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of infrastructure resilience literature and documented regional outages. We avoided extrapolating regional events to a global scenario.
