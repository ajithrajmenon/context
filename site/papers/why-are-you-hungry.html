Why are you hungry when you are not short of energy: the research

How appetite signalling works, and why energy balance is a poor model of hunger.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We set out to test the popular framing of hunger as a fuel gauge. It does not survive. Appetite is driven by hormonal signalling that is heavily influenced by habit, sleep and meal composition, which explains why identical calorie intakes produce very different hunger.

What we looked at

We set out to test the popular framing of hunger as a fuel gauge. It does not survive. Appetite is driven by hormonal signalling that is heavily influenced by habit, sleep and meal composition, which explains why identical calorie intakes produce very different hunger.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Ghrelin rises before habitual meal times.

    Well established

    Conditioned to schedule, not to depletion.

  2. 2

    Protein is the most satiating macronutrient per calorie.

    Well established

    Consistent across controlled feeding studies.

  3. 3

    Short sleep raises ghrelin and lowers leptin next day.

    Well established

    Replicated in restriction protocols.

  4. 4

    Leptin resistance contributes to sustained obesity.

    Best current explanation

    Mechanistically supported; causal role debated.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Do ultra-processed foods override satiety signalling?

One well-controlled inpatient trial found higher intake on ultra-processed diets at matched nutrients. It is one study, it is small, and it is being cited as though it were many.

What we could not establish

  • Why leptin responsiveness varies so much between individuals.
  • Whether appetite set points can be durably reset, or only temporarily shifted.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of endocrinology reviews and controlled feeding studies. We weighted inpatient controlled-feeding work above free-living observational data.

Further reading

Described so you know what each one is for, rather than listed to look thorough.

  1. Hall et al. (2019), Cell Metabolism

    The inpatient ultra-processed trial. Read the limitations section, which is unusually honest.

  2. Spiegel et al. (2004), on sleep and appetite hormones

    The origin of the sleep-appetite link.
