Why can you not tickle yourself: the research

Sensory attenuation of self-generated action, and its clinical relevance.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the forward-model account of why self-touch is attenuated. It is well supported experimentally, and its extension to auditory hallucination is plausible and actively researched rather than settled.

What we looked at

We examined the forward-model account of why self-touch is attenuated. It is well supported experimentally, and its extension to auditory hallucination is plausible and actively researched rather than settled.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Self-generated touch is attenuated relative to identical external touch.

    Well established

    Directly measured.

  2. 2

    Attenuation decreases as delay is introduced.

    Well established

    The robot-arm delay experiments.

  3. 3

    Attenuation is reduced in some people with schizophrenia.

    Best current explanation

    Replicated; interpretation debated.

  4. 4

    Misattribution of inner speech explains auditory hallucination.

    Contested

    Attractive and incomplete.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Is reduced attenuation a cause of hallucination or a correlate?

The dissociation is real. Whether it is upstream of symptoms, downstream, or both, is not resolved.

What we could not establish

  • Whether attenuation can be trained.
  • How specific the deficit is to hallucination rather than to psychosis generally.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of motor control and clinical neuroscience. We treated the delay-manipulation experiments as the strongest evidence because they are parametric.
