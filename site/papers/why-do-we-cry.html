Why do we cry: the research

The composition and social function of emotional tears.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined whether emotional crying has a function distinct from ocular maintenance. The signalling account is the best supported. The widely repeated claim that crying flushes stress chemicals from the body is much weaker than its popularity suggests.

What we looked at

We examined whether emotional crying has a function distinct from ocular maintenance. The signalling account is the best supported. The widely repeated claim that crying flushes stress chemicals from the body is much weaker than its popularity suggests.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Emotional tears differ in composition from basal and reflex tears.

    Well established

    Repeatedly measured.

  2. 2

    Visible tears change how observers interpret a face.

    Well established

    Demonstrated by tear-removal photograph studies.

  3. 3

    Crying with a supportive other produces more reported relief than crying alone.

    Well established

    Consistent across diary and survey work.

  4. 4

    Crying detoxifies the body of stress hormones.

    Contested

    Widely repeated, thinly evidenced.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Does crying itself improve mood?

Retrospective reports say yes; studies that watch people cry in a lab find mood is often worse immediately afterwards. Which measure to trust is the dispute.

What we could not establish

  • Why humans are apparently the only species that cries emotionally.
  • What the extra proteins in emotional tears actually do.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of affective science reviews. We separated laboratory measurement from retrospective self-report, because on this topic they disagree.

Further reading

Described so you know what each one is for, rather than listed to look thorough.

    The book-length case for the signalling account.

  2. Provine and colleagues on tear-removal studies

    The experiments that show tears carry information.
