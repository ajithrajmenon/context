Why do we get bored: the research

Boredom as an attentional signal, and the effects of chronic novelty availability.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We reviewed functional accounts of boredom. The evidence supports boredom as a meta-cognitive signal about attentional allocation rather than a state of low stimulation. Availability of low-effort novelty appears to alter its trajectory.

What we looked at

We reviewed functional accounts of boredom. The evidence supports boredom as a meta-cognitive signal about attentional allocation rather than a state of low stimulation. Availability of low-effort novelty appears to alter its trajectory.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Boredom occurs under both under-stimulation and over-availability of stimulus.

    Well established

    Consistent across experience-sampling.

  2. 2

    People will accept unpleasant stimulation to escape boredom.

    Well established

    Replicated shock-choice studies.

  3. 3

    Boredom reliably precedes mind-wandering.

    Well established

    Well documented.

  4. 4

    Boredom promotes creative output.

    Best current explanation

    Positive experimental results, modest effect sizes.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Whether smartphone use increases baseline boredom proneness.

The correlation is clear and the direction of causation is not.

What we could not establish

  • Why boredom proneness varies so widely between individuals.
  • Whether deliberately induced boredom has any therapeutic use.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of attention and motivation research. We distinguished state boredom from trait boredom proneness, because studies that mix them produce confused results.
