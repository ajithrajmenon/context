Why is the wifi bad in that one room: the research

Radio propagation indoors, material attenuation, and practical placement.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined why indoor wifi coverage correlates so poorly with distance. Material attenuation dominates, and typical placement wastes a large fraction of radiated power. Both are fixable without new hardware.

What we looked at

We examined why indoor wifi coverage correlates so poorly with distance. Material attenuation dominates, and typical placement wastes a large fraction of radiated power. Both are fixable without new hardware.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Wall material dominates indoor attenuation.

    Well established

    Standard propagation measurement.

  2. 2

    Water absorbs strongly at 2.4 GHz.

    Well established

    The same physics a microwave oven uses.

  3. 3

    5 GHz is faster and attenuates faster.

    Well established

    Basic frequency dependence.

  4. 4

    Central placement improves coverage substantially.

    Well established

    Follows directly from roughly isotropic radiation.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

How much interference from neighbouring networks matters in practice.

It varies enormously by density; measured congestion effects in flats are large and in detached housing often negligible.

What we could not establish

  • Typical real-world attenuation figures for modern building materials.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of RF propagation references and manufacturer guidance. Figures are indicative rather than precise.
