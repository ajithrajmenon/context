Why do you yawn, and why is it catching: the research

Mechanisms of yawning and the social gradient of yawn contagion.

August 2026 · about 7 minutes · the long layer behind the story

Abstract

We examined the thermoregulatory account of yawning and the evidence on contagion. Brain cooling is the best-supported mechanism. Contagion appears to be socially mediated and developmentally acquired rather than reflexive.

What we looked at

We examined the thermoregulatory account of yawning and the evidence on contagion. Brain cooling is the best-supported mechanism. Contagion appears to be socially mediated and developmentally acquired rather than reflexive.

Findings

Each one carries the confidence we are willing to defend it at. We use three levels only; more would be false precision.

  1. 1

    Manipulating inspired oxygen does not change yawning rate.

    Well established

    Directly tested.

  2. 2

    Yawning frequency falls when ambient temperature approaches body temperature.

    Well established

    Replicated across settings.

  3. 3

    Contagion strength scales with social closeness to the yawner.

    Well established

    Consistent across studies.

  4. 4

    Contagious yawning emerges around age four to five.

    Well established

    Developmental studies agree.

What is contested

Live disagreements, stated as disagreements rather than resolved for tidiness.

Whether brain cooling is the primary function or a side effect.

The correlations are solid; a causal demonstration in humans is not available.

What we could not establish

  • Why yawning is contagious across species in some cases.
  • What the pre-performance yawn in athletes is doing.

Method, and its limits

Synthesis of comparative and thermoregulatory research. Where a mechanism is inferred from correlation, we said so.
