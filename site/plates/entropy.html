Plate 06  /  Thermodynamics

Entropy is not messiness

Disorder is an aesthetic judgement and it makes no predictions. Entropy is a count — of how many microscopic arrangements produce the same macroscopic state. Once you count, the second law stops being a mystery and becomes arithmetic.

The usual picture
Systems tend toward disorder. Entropy is a measure of how messy things are, and the universe prefers mess.
What is actually happening
Entropy counts arrangements: S = k ln W. States that can be built in overwhelmingly more ways are overwhelmingly more likely to be found. Nothing prefers anything — there are just vastly more ways to be spread out.

The failure

Disorder explains nothing

Switch to the disorder reading and the readout returns adjectives. They cannot be added, differentiated, or fed into an engine calculation. And the intuition actively misleads: a crystallising liquid becomes visibly more ordered while total entropy rises, because the heat it dumps disorders the surroundings more.

Oil separating from water looks like spontaneous tidying. Life builds staggeringly ordered structures every second. None of this violates anything, but "disorder increases" gives you no way to see that.

The mechanism

Macrostates and microstates

Distinguish two descriptions. A microstate is the full specification: which molecule is where. A macrostate is what you can actually measure: how many are on the left.

Every microstate is equally likely. But macrostates are not, because they are built from wildly different numbers of microstates. There is exactly one way to put all hundred molecules on the left. There are about 1029 ways to split them fifty-fifty.

Boltzmann's S = k ln W is nothing more than that count, logged so it adds up sensibly across combined systems.

The correction

The second law is a statement about odds

Gas fills its container not because it dislikes corners but because the spread-out macrostates outnumber the concentrated ones so grotesquely that the alternative never comes up. With a hundred molecules the odds against all-on-one-side are 1030 to one. With a real roomful, the exponent runs to twenty-three digits.

So the second law is not a prohibition. Entropy decreasing is allowed, and in tiny systems it is observed. It is simply, for anything you can see, so improbable that the universe's age is not remotely long enough to wait for it.

Loose end

This reframes the arrow of time. Physics at the microscopic level runs the same forwards and backwards. What breaks the symmetry is that the universe began in an extremely low-entropy state. Everything we call "the past" is simply the direction in which there were fewer ways to be.
