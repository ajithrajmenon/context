Plate 09  /  Evolutionary biology

Evolution is not random, and organisms do not adapt because they need to

Both of the popular readings are wrong, and they are wrong in opposite directions. Variation is random. Survival is emphatically not. Evolution is the product of the two, and neither half alone produces anything.

The usual picture
Either it is all blind chance and complex life is an absurd fluke — or organisms sense what they need and change to acquire it.
What is actually happening
Mutation supplies variation blindly, with no regard to what would be useful. The environment then filters that variation non-randomly. Randomness proposes; selection disposes.

The failure

Two errors, both fatal

Run the chance-alone model. Mutation is working exactly as hard as in the other model, and the population never adapts — the mean trait staggers around at random. Randomness by itself is not a mechanism for building anything.

The opposite error fails just as cleanly. Organisms cannot detect what would be useful and produce it. Bacteria in an antibiotic do not sense the drug and invent resistance; the resistant ones were already there before the drug arrived, and the drug merely killed everything else.

The mechanism

The filter does the work

Selection needs three ingredients and no more: variation within the population, heritability of that variation, and differential survival or reproduction based on it. Supply all three and adaptation is not merely possible, it is unavoidable.

Notice where the randomness lives. It is confined to the generating step. The filtering step is ruthlessly systematic: it retains the same direction generation after generation, and that accumulation across thousands of generations is what looks like design.

This is why "evolution is just chance" mistakes one half of the process for the whole. It is like calling a sculpture random because the sculptor did not choose the grain of the stone.

The correction

Cumulative, not single-step

The intuition that complex adaptations are absurdly improbable is correct — if they had to appear in one jump. They do not. Each generation starts from the best of the last, so improbability is paid for in tiny instalments rather than all at once.

And the direction is set by the environment, not by need or effort. Change the background in the figure and the population would track it. Nothing wanted anything. The ones that happened to match simply left more offspring, and after twenty generations that is all it takes.

Loose end

Drift — the chance-alone model — is real and matters, especially in small populations, where useful traits can be lost by bad luck. Evolution is not selection alone. But drift produces change without direction, and it is direction that needs explaining.
