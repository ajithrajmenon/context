Plate 07  /  Relativity

Moving clocks are not malfunctioning

Every clock slows by exactly the same factor: atomic, mechanical, biological, radioactive. A disturbance that affected caesium atoms and pendulums and human ageing identically would be a strange disturbance. It is easier to accept that time is what is changing.

The usual picture
Motion jostles the mechanism. The clock reads wrong, but the real underlying time keeps ticking normally in the background.
What is actually happening
The speed of light is the same for every observer. If light must cover a longer diagonal path at the same speed, more time passes per tick — and there is no separate background time to appeal to.

The failure

What kind of disturbance is this?

If motion merely upset the mechanism, different mechanisms would be upset differently. A quartz oscillator and a decaying muon and a beating heart have nothing in common mechanically.

Yet all of them slow by the identical factor, and the factor depends only on speed. A "disturbance" that is blind to what it is disturbing and precisely predictable from velocity alone is not a disturbance. It is a property of the arena.

The mechanism

A longer path at a fixed speed

Start from the one experimental fact that forces everything else: light travels at c for every observer, regardless of how fast the source or the observer is moving. This is not an assumption; it is what Michelson and Morley found when they went looking for the alternative.

Now watch a light clock go past. In its own frame the photon bounces straight up and down. In yours, the mirrors have moved sideways between bounces, so the photon traces a diagonal. The diagonal is longer than the vertical.

Same speed, longer distance, more time per tick. That is the entire derivation, and the factor that falls out is 1/√(1−v²/c²).

The correction

There is no master clock

The instinct that survives longest is that one of the two clocks is really right. There is no such fact. Each observer sees the other's clock running slow, and both are correct, because simultaneity itself is frame-dependent.

The measurement is routine. Muons created in the upper atmosphere live about 2.2 microseconds — far too short to reach the ground even at nearly light speed. They arrive in quantity anyway, because from our frame their internal clocks are dilated. GPS satellites must correct for both this and gravitational dilation or navigation drifts by kilometres a day.

Loose end

The twin who leaves and returns really does come back younger, and the asymmetry is not paradoxical: only one twin accelerated, so only one changed frames. The situation was never symmetric, which is exactly why the outcome is not either.
