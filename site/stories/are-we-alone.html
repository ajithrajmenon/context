Life

If the universe is so big, where is everybody?

Our galaxy has been around long enough for a civilisation to cross it many times over. By every reasonable estimate, someone should have arrived by now. Nobody has.

  1. The numbers are absurd in our favour

    Our galaxy holds a few hundred billion stars. Most have planets. Many of those planets sit at a comfortable distance from their star.

    And the galaxy is old. If even one species had started spreading a million years ago, they would already be everywhere.

  2. So the sky should be busy. It is not.

    No signals. No probes. No evidence of anything, anywhere, ever. That gap between what we expect and what we find is the whole problem.

  3. Something stops civilisations from filling the galaxy. We do not know what, or whether we have passed it yet.

  4. Where the barrier could be

    1. 1

      Behind us

      Starting life at all may be a fluke so rare it has happened once. If so, we are alone, and we are safe.

    2. 2

      Also behind us

      Simple cells took billions of years to become complex ones. That step may almost never happen.

    3. 3

      Ahead of us

      Every civilisation might destroy itself shortly after learning how. This is the version worth worrying about.

  6. Silence is not comforting

    If we ever find simple life on Mars or under the ice of Europa, that means life is easy — and the hard step is probably still in front of us.

    The best news would be finding nothing at all. An empty galaxy might mean we already got through the difficult part.

  7. Zoom out

    Either we are alone in an unimaginably large place, or we are not. Both answers are enormous, and we are the first generation with instruments good enough to start ruling one of them out.
