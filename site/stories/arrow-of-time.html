Why can you remember yesterday but not tomorrow?

Film a pendulum and run it backwards and nothing looks wrong. Film a cup falling and run it backwards and it is obviously false. Both are governed by the same laws.

3 min read · you will come away knowing where the direction of time actually comes from.

  1. The laws do not contain a direction

    Newton's mechanics, electromagnetism, general relativity and quantum mechanics are all essentially reversible. Nothing in the equations distinguishes forward from backward. Yet every experience you have is one-directional. That gap is one of the deepest open questions in physics, and it is hiding in a smashed cup.

  2. Almost every law of physics runs equally well backwards. Your life does not.

  4. It is about counting, not about time

    There are very few arrangements of atoms that constitute an intact cup and an unimaginable number that constitute fragments. Nothing forbids fragments reassembling; it is simply that overwhelmingly more futures lead to mess than to order. Disorder increases not because a law compels it but because there is vastly more of it to fall into.

  5. Time does not flow. Things spread out, and the direction in which they spread is the direction we call forward.

  7. Which pushes the question back one step

    Statistics explains why disorder increases from a low-disorder state, but it does not explain why the universe started in one. As far as anyone can tell, the early universe was in an extraordinarily improbable low-entropy condition, and every arrow of time we experience — including memory, cause preceding effect, and ageing — traces back to that single unexplained initial fact.

  8. Zoom out

    You remember yesterday and not tomorrow because 13.8 billion years ago the universe began in a state so orderly that nobody has been able to explain it. Your entire sense of time passing is downstream of that.
