Why does a 99% accurate test get it wrong most of the time?

A test is 99% accurate. You test positive. The chance you actually have the condition can easily be under 10%, and almost nobody finds that intuitive.

3 min read · you will come away knowing how to read any test result, medical or otherwise, in one step.

  1. Accuracy sounds like the answer

    If a test is right 99 times out of 100, a positive result feels like it should be right 99 times out of 100. That reasoning skips the single most important number: how common the condition is to begin with.

  2. For a rare condition, most positive results are false. Accuracy is not the number that matters.

  4. The rarity dominates

    There are so many more healthy people that even a small error rate applied to all of them produces more false positives than there are true cases. The test did nothing wrong. The arithmetic simply is not what intuition expects.

  5. A positive result does not tell you the chance you are ill. It tells you to multiply by how likely you were before the test.

  7. Where this bites

    This is why population screening for rare conditions produces so much anxiety and so many follow-up procedures, and why doctors test people with symptoms rather than everybody.

  8. Zoom out

    The most consequential number in any test result is the one that was true before the test was taken.
