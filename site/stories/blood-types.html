Why do we have blood types?

Before 1901, transfusion killed about as often as it saved and nobody knew why. The answer turned out to be a difference in decoration.

3 min read · you will come away knowing what your type does and does not mean.

  1. The type is not the blood

    Your red cells are studded with molecules, and on the ends of some of them sit short chains of sugars. Type A carries one arrangement, type B another, AB both, and O carries the unmodified base version. That is the entire difference. Everything else about the blood is the same.

  2. Sugar coatings on your cells, discovered a century ago, and still not fully explained.

  4. Your immune system has already picked a side

    You carry antibodies against whichever sugars you do not have, and you have them from infancy without ever having encountered foreign blood — most likely because similar sugars appear on common gut bacteria. This is why the wrong transfusion is catastrophic immediately: the antibodies are already loaded and waiting.

  5. Blood types are not blood varieties. They are sugar coatings, and your immune system decided its position on them before you were a year old.

  7. What we still do not know

    Why the system exists at all is not settled. Different types show small differences in susceptibility to particular infections — the clearest case is severe malaria, where type O appears somewhat protected — which suggests the variation is maintained by disease pressure. That is a good hypothesis rather than a finished answer. What is settled is that the blood type diet has no evidence behind it, and has been tested directly.

  8. Zoom out

    A century after saving millions of lives, we still cannot say confidently what blood types are for. Knowing how to use something is not the same as understanding it.
