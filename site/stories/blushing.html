Why do we blush?

You cannot blush on command, you cannot stop one, and everybody can see it. As a piece of design that seems like pure sabotage.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why the thing you are ashamed of may be working for you.

  1. It is a genuine loss of control

    Blushing runs on the sympathetic nervous system through blood vessels in the face, neck and upper chest. There is no voluntary route to it. Actors cannot produce one. People desperate to suppress theirs make it worse, because the attempt raises the arousal driving it.

  2. The only human expression that cannot be faked, and the only one that gives you away.

  4. Which is exactly why it is trusted

    A signal that anyone can produce at will is worthless, because liars produce it too. A signal that nobody can fake carries real information. Experiments find that people who visibly blush after a social violation are judged more trustworthy, are forgiven more readily, and are more likely to be given a second chance than people who apologise with a straight face.

  5. Blushing is not a malfunction. It is a confession your body makes on your behalf, and it is believed precisely because you did not choose to make it.

  7. The cost, and who pays it

    None of this consoles anyone with severe blushing anxiety, which is genuinely disabling and traps people in exactly the loop described. But knowing what the signal does helps: the thing you are trying to hide is the thing making other people more inclined to like you.

  8. Zoom out

    Some of our most uncomfortable equipment is uncomfortable on purpose. A signal that cost you nothing would tell nobody anything.
