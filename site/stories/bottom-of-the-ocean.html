What lives at the bottom of the ocean?

Sunlight gives up at about two hundred metres. The sea keeps going for another eleven thousand, and every layer below the light has something living in it.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why the largest habitat on Earth is the one we have looked at least.

  1. Almost all of the ocean is dark

    The bright blue part you can picture is a thin skin on top. Below two hundred metres there is not enough light for a plant. That dark part is most of the living space on this planet, by a very long way.

  2. Eleven kilometres down, in permanent dark under crushing pressure, things are still alive.

  4. So they brought their own light

    Below about a kilometre most of what you would see makes its own light — for hunting, for hiding, and for finding each other. Bioluminescence is not exotic down there; it is the norm.

  5. More people have stood on the Moon than have been to the deepest point in the sea.

  7. Why this changed where we look for life

    It is a large part of why the icy moons of Jupiter and Saturn, with dark oceans under their shells, are now serious places to go looking.

  8. Zoom out

    We have better maps of Mars than of our own sea floor. The largest habitat on this planet is the one we have examined least.
