Numbers

What if everyone jumped at once?

Eight billion people is a genuinely enormous number, and it produces an effect too small to name in ordinary units. That gap is the interesting part.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why our sense of a very large number tops out early.

  1. Eight billion sounds like enough

    Add up every human alive and you get around half a billion tonnes. The intuition says that much mass, moving together, must do something.

  2. The Earth would move less than the width of an atom, and then move back.

  4. The Earth does push back

    Momentum is conserved, so the planet genuinely recoils. It recoils by an amount that has no business being called a distance — a small fraction of the width of a single atom.

  5. The planet would shift by a fraction of an atom's width, and then shift straight back. The jump is a rounding error.

  7. Why the question feels bigger than it is

    Our sense of a lot saturates early. Past a certain point, numbers stop feeling proportionally larger, so eight billion and eight trillion feel similar. They are not.

  8. Zoom out

    The question feels like it should have a dramatic answer because our intuition for scale gave up several orders of magnitude ago.
