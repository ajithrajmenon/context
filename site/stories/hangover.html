Why does a hangover hurt so much?

A hangover is one of the most reliably reproducible states in human experience, and almost everything commonly said about it is wrong.

3 min read · you will come away knowing which remedies have evidence and which are folklore.

  1. The dehydration story is doing too much work

    Alcohol does suppress the hormone that tells your kidneys to hold water, and you do lose fluid. But rehydration studies find that replacing that fluid improves the headache only slightly and leaves the rest of the misery essentially untouched.

  2. Mostly not dehydration. Water was never going to fix it.

  4. Your body is mounting a small immune response

    Metabolising alcohol produces acetaldehyde, which is toxic and which your body clears in a second step. Meanwhile the immune system releases signalling molecules — the same ones that make you feel wretched with flu. That is where the aching, the fog and the sensitivity to light come from. Alcohol also wrecks the second half of the night's sleep, so you are running the whole thing sleep-deprived.

  5. A hangover is mostly a small inflammatory illness, layered on a bad night's sleep and a rebound in a nervous system that spent the evening suppressed.

  7. Why nothing works

    There is no cure because there is no single cause. You are dealing with inflammation, a toxic metabolite, disrupted sleep and neurotransmitter rebound at the same time, and fixing any one of them leaves the other three running.

  8. Zoom out

    It is a useful example of a problem with four causes. Anything sold as a single fix for it is selling you a story.
