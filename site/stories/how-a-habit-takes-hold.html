How does a habit take hold?

People who sincerely intend to change a behaviour, and can explain exactly why they should, reliably fail to. That is not a character finding. It is an architecture finding.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why changing the cue beats trying harder, every time.

  1. Willpower is the wrong frame

    The intuitive model is a single decision-maker who is sometimes too weak. If that were right, motivation would predict change. It predicts it surprisingly poorly.

  2. Habits are not weak intentions. They are a different memory system, and intention cannot reach it.

  4. Habits run on cues

    A habit is a learned link from a context to an action. The reward built the link, but once built, the cue fires the action regardless of whether the reward still exists.

  5. You cannot argue with a habit, because the part of you making the argument is not the part performing the behaviour.

  7. What follows

    Changing context breaks habits more reliably than resolve does, which is why moving house or changing jobs is such a common moment for behaviour to shift. The window is real and it closes.

  8. Zoom out

    Habits are not a failure of character. They are a system doing precisely what it was built to do, in a situation nobody consulted it about.
