Numbers

How big is a billion?

You can say million, billion and trillion in the same breath, which is exactly the problem. They sound like neighbours.

3 min read · you will come away knowing a way of holding large numbers that actually works.

  1. The words are too similar

    One letter separates them and each step multiplies by a thousand. Written out, a billion is a thousand millions and a trillion is a thousand billions. Reading those sentences does nothing; the numbers stay flat and adjacent in your head.

  2. A million seconds is eleven days. A billion seconds is thirty-two years.

  4. Your sense of number is logarithmic

    Humans do not perceive quantity linearly. Children asked to place numbers on a line put small ones far apart and crowd the large ones together, and formal schooling only partly trains this out. Beyond a few thousand, everything compresses into a single category labelled a lot. That is why a million and a billion feel like the same kind of thing. They differ by a factor of a thousand.

  5. You do not have an intuition for large numbers. You have an intuition for the shape of the word, and every large number is roughly the same shape.

  7. Why this is not a party trick

    Public argument runs on these numbers constantly — budgets, debts, populations, fortunes. If a million and a billion sit in the same mental bin, you cannot evaluate almost any claim about public money, and you are open to being led by whoever chooses the units. Converting to time is the cheapest available defence.

  8. Zoom out

    Convert to seconds. It is a single arithmetic step and it turns a word you cannot feel into a duration you can.
