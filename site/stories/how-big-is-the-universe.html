How big is the universe?

The universe is 13.8 billion years old and the edge of what we can see is 46 billion light years away. Both figures are correct, and reconciling them means giving up a picture you have probably carried since childhood.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why the observable universe is wider than its own age allows.

  1. The explosion picture is wrong

    The Big Bang is usually imagined as matter flying outward from a point into surrounding emptiness. There was no surrounding emptiness and no point to fly from. Every location we can see was involved. Wherever you stand, everything appears to be receding from you, and that is true for every observer everywhere.

  2. Space is not expanding into anything. The distances themselves are growing.

  4. Which is why the numbers do not add up

    Light from the most distant thing we can see set out 13.8 billion years ago. While it travelled, the space it had already crossed kept stretching, so its point of origin is now about 46 billion light years away. Nothing outran light. The road got longer under it.

  5. The universe is not big because things flew a long way. It is big because the distances themselves grew while the light was still in transit.

  7. And the view is closing

    Expansion is accelerating, so galaxies now visible will eventually recede faster than their light can reach us and will fade permanently from view. Astronomers in the far future will see a single galaxy in an apparently static void, with no evidence that anything else ever existed. We happen to be living in the window when the history is still readable.

  8. Zoom out

    We are not at the centre of the universe, and we are near the middle of the only period in which it is possible to work out that we are not.
