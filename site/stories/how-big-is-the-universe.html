Space

How big is the universe, really?

Look up on a clear night and you are looking backwards in time. The further out you look, the older the picture — and there is a hard edge to how far the looking can go.

  1. Light is fast, but it is not instant

    Sunlight takes eight minutes to reach you. You never see the Sun as it is, only as it was.

    For distant galaxies that delay becomes billions of years. You are looking at an old photograph, not a live feed.

  2. The universe is 13.8 billion years old

    So no light has had time to travel further than 13.8 billion years to get here. Anything beyond that has simply not arrived yet.

  3. But the edge is 46 billion light years away

    That sounds like a mistake. It is not. While the light was travelling, space itself was stretching, carrying its starting point further away behind it.

    The light took 13.8 billion years. The place it left is now 46 billion light years off.

  4. Space is not expanding into anything. There is no outside. It is the distances themselves that are growing.

  5. Three different "sizes" people mean

    1. 1

      What we can see

      A ball 46 billion light years in every direction. Everything we will ever have evidence of.

    2. 2

      What we can reach

      Far smaller. Beyond about 16 billion light years, space grows faster than we could ever chase it.

    3. 3

      The whole thing

      Unknown, and possibly infinite. We are inside it, so we cannot step back and measure.

  7. And the reachable part is shrinking

    Expansion is speeding up. Every year, a few more galaxies drift permanently out of range. The universe we could ever touch is quietly getting smaller.

  8. Zoom out

    We arrived early enough to see it. Anyone looking up in a hundred billion years will see an empty sky and have no way of knowing there was ever anything else. Being able to ask this question is itself a piece of luck.
