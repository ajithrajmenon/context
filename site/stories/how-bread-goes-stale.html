How does bread go stale?

Wrap a loaf so it cannot lose a gram of water and it still goes stale. Whatever staling is, it is not drying out.

3 min read · you will come away knowing where to actually put bread, and why toasting revives it.

  1. The drying story is wrong

    Bread in a sealed bag stales on schedule. The water is still there — it has moved. Starch that baking forced into a loose, disordered arrangement slowly settles back into ordered crystals, and those crystals grab the water and stiffen the crumb.

  2. Stale is not dry. It is starch recrystallising, and that happens fastest just above freezing.

  4. The fridge sits in the worst zone

    Recrystallisation runs fastest a little above freezing — which is exactly the temperature of your fridge. Below freezing the molecules barely move, so a freezer nearly stops the clock.

  5. Chilling bread does not preserve it. It accelerates the one process that ruins it.

  7. Where to put it

    Eating it within two days: counter, in paper or a bread bin. Longer: slice it and freeze it, then toast from frozen. The fridge is for sandwiches, not loaves.

  8. Zoom out

    One of the most repeated pieces of kitchen advice is precisely backwards, and the correct version costs nothing to adopt.
