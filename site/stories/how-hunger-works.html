Your body

How does your body decide you are hungry?

A person carrying plenty of stored energy can be ravenous. A person who has just eaten can be hungry an hour later. If hunger measured fuel, neither would happen.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why what you eat changes appetite more than how much you count.

  1. Hunger feels like a measurement

    The intuitive model is a tank with a gauge. Energy goes down, hunger goes up, you eat, the gauge resets.

    If that were true, appetite would track energy stores. It tracks them poorly, and the mismatch is not a personal failing.

  2. Hunger is not a fuel gauge. It is a set of hormone signals, and they can be wrong.

  4. The signals can be argued with

    Ghrelin rises in anticipation of meals you habitually eat. Leptin reports long-term stores but the brain can become less responsive to it.

    So hunger is a negotiation between habit, hormones, sleep, protein intake and what is in front of you — not a readout.

  5. Appetite is not measuring what is in the tank. It is a prediction, and predictions can be trained.

  7. What to do with this

    Front-loading protein and fibre reduces later hunger measurably, which is why the same calorie count can feel completely different.

    One night of short sleep raises ghrelin and lowers leptin the next day. A great deal of what people read as weak discipline is a hormone report from a bad night.

  8. Zoom out

    You are not failing to override a gauge. You are negotiating with a prediction system that was tuned for a world with less available food, and it can be re-tuned.
