Time

How does the universe end?

The universe is 13.8 billion years old, which sounds ancient. On the timeline of how long it has left, it has barely started.

  1. Stars are a phase, not a feature

    Stars need gas to form from, and every star burns some of it permanently into heavier stuff.

    There is a fixed budget. In about a hundred trillion years the last star will form, burn, and go out. After that, no new ones. Ever.

  2. The lights go out one at a time

    The small red ones last longest. The last of them will still be glowing faintly a thousand times longer than the universe has existed so far.

  3. What comes after the lights

    1. 1

      The dark era

      Cold leftovers drifting: dead stars, dead planets, black holes. Nothing shines.

    2. 2

      The black holes eat

      They pull in what is left and become the only structures of any size in the universe.

    3. 3

      The black holes leak

      Even they evaporate, unbelievably slowly. The largest take longer than any number worth writing down.

  4. Everything ends the same way: spread out, cooled down, and too even for anything to happen.

  6. This is not a sad story yet

    We are in the brightest, busiest, most eventful slice the universe will ever have. Stars are burning, planets are forming, chemistry is happening.

    By any measure of time, we turned up at the beginning, while the lights were still on.

  7. Zoom out

    Everything ends. It just takes so long that the ending is not really the point. The point is that for one brief, absurdly early moment there was enough going on for something to sit up and notice.
