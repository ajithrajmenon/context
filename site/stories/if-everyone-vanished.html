Earth

What if every human vanished tomorrow?

Not an apocalypse — just everybody gone, everything else left exactly as it is. What happens next is a good way of finding out how much of the world is being actively held in place.

  1. Hours: the lights go out

    Most power stations need someone to feed them. Within hours the grid fails, and almost everywhere goes dark that first night.

    For the first time in two centuries, you could see the stars from the middle of a city.

  2. The night sky comes back immediately

    No lights, no aircraft, no noise. Within a day the planet sounds and looks the way it did for the previous few hundred thousand years.

  3. Then it is just a matter of waiting

    1. 1

      Days

      Pumps stop. Underground railways flood. Livestock and pets are on their own.

    2. 2

      Years

      Water gets into every roof. Frost cracks it wider. Plants take the roads apart from underneath.

    3. 3

      Decades

      Wooden buildings collapse. Fires run unchecked through cities. Rivers go back to their old courses.

    4. 4

      Centuries

      Steel corrodes through and the tall buildings come down. Forest covers most of what was city.

  4. Nature does not have to fight the city. It only has to wait, and it is extremely good at waiting.

  6. Some of it recovers faster than you would think

    Places we have already abandoned fill with wildlife within decades. Not because they are clean, but because we are not there.

  7. What actually outlasts us

    Not the buildings. Plastic in the sediment, a thin layer of odd chemistry in the rock, and the radio signals already on their way out.

    And the footprints on the Moon, which have no wind or water to remove them and could plausibly still be there in ten million years.

  8. Zoom out

    Everything around you is being actively held in place. Stop holding, and the world does not end — it just carries on without the maintenance, and gets on perfectly well.
