What if the Sun vanished right now?

Suppose the Sun simply stopped existing this second. The surprise is not what happens. It is how long absolutely nothing happens for.

  1. The light is old news

    Sunlight takes about eight minutes and twenty seconds to cross the gap to Earth. The light already in flight keeps coming.

    For eight minutes the sky stays bright, the day stays warm, and nobody on Earth has any way of knowing.

  2. Gravity is not instant either

    This is the part that trips people up. Gravity travels at exactly the same speed as light. Not faster. Not instantly.

  3. Earth would keep circling a Sun that was not there — for the same eight minutes, to the second.

  4. Then both arrive together

    At eight minutes twenty, the sky goes dark and Earth stops turning its corner at the same instant.

    We would not fly outward, exactly. We would go straight — off along whatever direction we happened to be travelling, in a line, forever.

  6. Why gravity has a speed at all

    Gravity is not a rope pulling you. It is a dent in space, and a dent has to spread outward like a ripple. Remove the weight and the ripple takes time to arrive.

  7. Zoom out

    Nothing in the universe finds out about anything instantly. Every fact has to travel, and none of it travels faster than light. The present moment is strictly local, and everything else is news.
