Why can you not remember being a baby?

You spent two or three years learning to walk, talk and recognise faces, and you cannot recall a single moment of it.

3 min read · you will come away knowing when your continuous memory actually begins, and why the earliest ones are often borrowed.

  1. It is not that infants cannot remember

    Babies form memories constantly. A six-month-old can learn to kick a mobile and remember the trick for weeks. Toddlers remember routines, people and places perfectly well. Whatever is missing later, it is not memory formation.

  2. Not because nothing happened. The filing system had not been built.

  4. The index is what is missing

    Autobiographical memory needs more than storage. It needs a sense of a continuing self that things happen to, and a language for sequence and time. Both arrive around three or four, and the hippocampus is still reorganising heavily before then. Memories laid down without an index are not retrievable, in much the same way a book with no catalogue entry is still in the library and still cannot be found.

  5. The memories are not deleted. They were filed before there was anyone to file them under.

  7. Which is why the earliest one is suspect

    Ask people for their first memory and most name something around age three, often with a photograph or a family story attached to it. Studies that check against records find a large share of these are reconstructions assembled from being told, and are held with full confidence. Your first memory may well be someone else's.

  8. Zoom out

    The first years of your life shaped almost everything about you and left you no record of it. You are, in the most literal sense, the result of events you cannot access.
