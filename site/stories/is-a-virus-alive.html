Is a virus alive?

A virus sits exactly on the line biology drew around life, and has not moved off it in a century of argument.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why the argument was never about viruses.

  1. Instructions in a box

    Strip one down and you find a short set of genetic instructions in a protein shell. No mouth, no engine, no metabolism. Left alone it does nothing at all, indefinitely.

  2. It cannot eat, move or reproduce alone. It is also extraordinarily good at what it does.

  4. It only becomes anything inside a cell

    The shell docks, the instructions go in, and the cell begins manufacturing copies of the thing infecting it. The virus contributes information; the cell contributes everything else.

  5. A virus is not alive and not dead. It is a set of instructions waiting for a machine that can read them.

  7. The category was ours, not nature's

    Nature did not agree to our definition. We drew a line called alive, and viruses sat down on top of it.

  8. Zoom out

    The question turns out to be about the sharpness of a word rather than the nature of a thing — which is the most common way a scientific argument stays open for a hundred years.
