Why do leaves change colour?

An autumn tree looks like it is doing something dramatic. Most of what you are seeing is something stopping.

3 min read · you will come away knowing how to read a tree's year off the colour of its leaves.

  1. Green is expensive, and it goes first

    Chlorophyll is the molecule that turns light into sugar, and it degrades constantly in sunlight, so a tree has to keep rebuilding it all summer. As days shorten and cold arrives, the tree stops paying. Chlorophyll breaks down and is not replaced.

  2. The colours were there all summer. The green was covering them.

  4. The yellows were never hidden by accident

    Carotenoids sit alongside chlorophyll all year doing real work: they capture light at wavelengths chlorophyll misses, and they mop up damaging molecules produced when a leaf takes more light than it can use. You have been looking at them since spring. They were simply outvoted.

  5. The tree is not painting the leaf. It is withdrawing the green and letting you see what was underneath the whole time.

  7. The red is still an open question

    Manufacturing anthocyanin in a leaf you are about to drop is a real cost. The leading explanation is sunscreen: the red pigment shields the leaf while the tree extracts the last of its nitrogen, which it will need next spring. A rival explanation says the colour is a signal to insects deciding where to lay eggs. Both have support. Neither has won.

  8. Zoom out

    Autumn is not a display. It is a shutdown, run carefully, with the valuables removed first — and one part of it that we still cannot fully explain.
