What is happening when a limb falls asleep?

Sit awkwardly for ten minutes and a leg goes numb, then floods with pins and needles as you stand. Almost everyone explains this wrongly, and the wrong explanation is the reassuring one.

3 min read · you will come away knowing when it is harmless, and the specific signs that mean it is not.

  1. The blood story is mostly wrong

    The usual account is that circulation was cut off and the tingling is blood rushing back. Compression does reduce blood flow, but that is not what produces the sensation.

    What actually happens is direct mechanical pressure on the nerve, which stops it conducting signals properly.

  2. The tingling is not blood returning. It is nerves misfiring as pressure comes off them.

  4. Recovery is noisy because it is uneven

    Nerve fibres do not all recover at the same rate. For a period, some are firing spontaneously while others are still silent.

    Your brain receives a burst of uncoordinated signals and has no way to interpret it except as the prickling everyone recognises.

  5. Pins and needles are not circulation returning. They are hundreds of nerve fibres coming back online at slightly different times.

  7. Why it matters to know the difference

    Persistent compression damages nerves for real. Carpal tunnel and ulnar neuropathy both begin as sensations people dismiss as a limb falling asleep.

    The useful rule is duration and repetition. Minutes after an odd posture is normal. Nightly, one-sided, or with lasting weakness is worth investigating.

  8. Zoom out

    The most common explanation for one of the most common sensations in the body is wrong, and the correct one comes with a practical rule attached.
