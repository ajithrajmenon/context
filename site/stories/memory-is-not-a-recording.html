Why are confident memories so often wrong?

People recall where they were when they heard shocking news with total conviction. Checked against what they wrote down at the time, a large share of those memories are wrong in detail.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why confidence and accuracy are almost unrelated, and what that means for testimony.

  1. It feels like playback

    Remembering feels like retrieving a file. Nothing about the experience suggests construction, which is exactly why the error is so hard to notice.

  2. Memory is not a recording. It is rebuilt each time you use it, and re-saved with the changes.

  4. Confidence comes from fluency

    Your sense of certainty tracks how easily the memory assembled, not whether it is right. A well-rehearsed false memory assembles very easily indeed.

  5. You do not remember the event. You remember the last time you remembered it.

  7. Where this has consequences

    Eyewitness misidentification is a leading contributing factor in wrongful convictions later overturned by DNA. Confident, sincere, and wrong is a coherent combination.

  8. Zoom out

    The thing you trust most about your own past is a reconstruction, and its confidence is a measure of practice rather than truth.
