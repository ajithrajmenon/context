You are mostly not you

You are carrying roughly as many bacterial cells as human ones. They are not along for the ride: a good deal of what you think of as your body is work they are doing.

3 min read · you will come away knowing where they actually live, and what they are doing for you.

  1. The famous ratio was wrong

    For decades the figure was ten bacteria per human cell. It came from a back-of-an-envelope estimate in 1972 that nobody re-checked for forty years.

    Done properly, the answer is roughly one to one — about thirty trillion human cells and thirty-eight trillion bacteria. Still an extraordinary number of tenants.

  2. About half the cells you are carrying are not human, and they are not passengers.

  4. They weigh almost nothing

    All of them together come to about two hundred grams — a small apple. Bacteria are so much smaller than your cells that tens of trillions barely register on a scale.

  5. You are not an individual. You are a negotiation, and most of the other party lives in your large intestine.

  7. Why this changed medicine

    Faecal transplant for recurrent C. difficile works, with cure rates well above antibiotics. That is the strongest clinical evidence that the community itself is doing something, not just sitting there.

  8. Zoom out

    Nothing complicated on this planet made it alone. The boundary you draw around yourself is a convenience rather than a fact.
