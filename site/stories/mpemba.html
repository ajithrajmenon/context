Can hot water freeze faster than cold?

A schoolboy in Tanzania noticed it in 1963 while making ice cream, was told he was wrong, and turned out to have found something the field still cannot close.

3 min read · you will come away knowing what an unresolved argument looks like from inside.

  1. It should be impossible

    Hot water has to pass through every temperature the cold water starts at. The obvious conclusion is that it can never arrive first. That argument is clean, intuitive, and treats the water as a single number, which it is not.

  2. Sometimes, apparently. Nobody can say reliably when, or why.

  4. The problem is the question

    Freezing is not one event. Do you mean when ice first appears, when the last liquid solidifies, or when the sample reaches a set temperature? Different definitions give different answers from the same experiment. Add the fact that water can sit well below zero without freezing at all, and depends heavily on container, stirring and impurities, and the result becomes very hard to pin down. The most careful attempts to reproduce it have often failed.

  5. The effect is real enough to keep being reported and slippery enough that nobody has managed to state the conditions under which it happens.

  7. Why this one is worth knowing about

    Most science you meet is finished, presented as a clean result with the argument sanded off. This one is still in progress, over something as ordinary as a cup of water, and it is a useful calibration: if this can stay open for sixty years, be careful with confident claims about harder things.

  8. Zoom out

    The interesting part is not whether hot water freezes faster. It is that a question this simple can resist an answer for six decades, in public, with everyone watching.
