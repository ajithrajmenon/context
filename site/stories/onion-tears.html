Why does cutting an onion make you cry?

An intact onion is harmless. You can hold one to your face all day. The weapon does not exist until you cut.

3 min read · you will come away knowing the two things that actually work, and why the rest are theatre.

  1. It is not in the onion

    The onion stores a harmless sulphur compound in one place and an enzyme in another, kept apart. Cutting breaks the cells and lets them meet. Within seconds a reaction produces a volatile molecule that did not exist before your knife arrived. It drifts up, reaches the film of moisture on your eye, and forms a mild acid.

  2. The onion is defending itself, and most of the kitchen tricks do nothing.

  4. Your eye is doing exactly the right thing

    The nerve endings in your cornea are among the most sensitive on the body, and detecting a mild acid there triggers reflex tearing to dilute and flush it. This is not the same fluid as emotional tears and does not involve any emotional machinery. It is a wash cycle.

  5. The onion is not making you cry. It is releasing a chemical weapon on being wounded, and your eye is rinsing it off.

  7. Why the onion bothers

    This is a defence against being eaten, aimed at animals that dig. It works on a great many of them. It fails against the one species that responded by inventing sharp knives, cold storage and extractor fans, and then cultivated the onion deliberately across most of the planet.

  8. Zoom out

    A plant evolved a chemical weapon so effective that we now grow a hundred million tonnes of it a year specifically to set the weapon off.
