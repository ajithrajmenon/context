Why does a paper cut hurt so much?

A paper cut is trivially small and disproportionately awful, and both facts have the same explanation.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why where you are hurt matters more than how badly.

  1. Pain is not proportional to damage

    Your brain has no direct access to tissue damage. It has access to nerve signals, and nerve endings are distributed extremely unevenly across the body. The fingertips carry one of the densest concentrations anywhere, because a hand that cannot feel is a hand that cannot be trusted.

  2. A wound you can barely see, out-hurting one that needed stitches.

  4. And the wound refuses to settle

    Paper tears rather than slices. Under magnification the edge is ragged, so the cut is a shallow, wide field of damage rather than a clean line. It is too shallow to bleed much, and bleeding is part of how a wound seals itself. Then you keep using the finger, which reopens it all day.

  5. It hurts because of where it is, not what it is. The densest sensor field on your body has been given a ragged wound that never gets to close.

  7. What this tells you about pain in general

    Pain is a warning system with priorities, and its priorities are about protecting function rather than reporting damage accurately. It is loudest where your body most needs you to pay attention. That is also why some serious injuries are famously painless in the moment.

  8. Zoom out

    You do not have a sensor for injury. You have a sensor for what your body has decided is worth interrupting you about.
