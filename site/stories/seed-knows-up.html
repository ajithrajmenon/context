How does a seed know which way is up?

Plant a seed upside down and the root still goes down and the shoot still goes up. Something inside it is measuring direction.

3 min read · you will come away knowing how a plant senses gravity, and what happens when you take it away.

  1. It cannot be light

    A seed several centimetres down is in total darkness, and it commits to a direction before it reaches the surface. Turn a germinating seedling on its side in a dark box and within hours the root bends downward and the shoot bends up. Whatever it is using, it is available underground.

  2. It has no eyes and is buried in the dark. It gets it right every time.

  4. It is a spirit level made of starch

    Certain cells in the root tip contain starch grains dense enough to sink through the cell. They settle on whichever side is down. The cell detects where they have landed and redirects a hormone, auxin, toward that side. Auxin slows growth in root cells, so the underside grows more slowly than the top, and the root curves downward. In the shoot, auxin has the opposite effect, so the same signal bends it up.

  5. A seed does not know which way is up. It has grains of starch that fall, and a growth response wired to where they land.

  7. Take gravity away and it shows

    Plants grown on the space station, with the starch grains floating rather than settling, lose reliable root direction and fall back on other cues — moisture, touch, light. It is the clean confirmation: remove the falling grains and the sense of down goes with them.

  8. Zoom out

    One of the most reliable behaviours in biology runs on nothing more clever than something heavy falling in a small room. A great deal of life is built this way.
