Why does the shower curtain attack you?

Every shower, the curtain reaches in and sticks to your leg. The explanation people give involves heat, and it is testably wrong.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why it happens with cold water too, and what actually stops it.

  1. It is not the hot air rising

    Warm air rising does contribute, and a hot shower usually pulls harder. But run the shower cold and the curtain still comes in. If heat were the cause, cold water would leave it alone.

  2. Falling water thins the air inside, and the room pushes the curtain in. Nothing pulls it.

  4. Moving air presses less

    Thousands of falling drops drag air down with them and fling it outward near the floor. Air that is moving quickly presses on its surroundings slightly less than still air does, so the inside of the curtain is pressed less hard than the outside.

  5. Nothing is pulling the curtain. The air inside got thinner, and the room pushed it in.

  7. Why the small pressure wins

    The difference is tiny — a fraction of a percent. But it acts over a large sheet, and a curtain weighs almost nothing. Small force, large area, negligible mass.

  8. Zoom out

    A trivial nuisance turns out to be a clean demonstration that pressure differences do not need to be large to move things that are light.
