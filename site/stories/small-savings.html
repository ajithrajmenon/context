How does a little money become a lot?

Money set aside earns a little. Then that little earns too. For years it looks like nothing is happening, and then the gap opens fast.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why starting earlier beats saving more, and by how much.

  1. It looks linear for a long time

    In year one the interest is trivial. In year five it is still small. Nothing about the early experience suggests what the curve does later, which is why so many people stop.

  2. The part doing the work is the years, not the amount.

  4. The growing part grows on itself

    Halfway along, the interest starts earning more each year than you are contributing. That crossover is the whole phenomenon, and it is invisible until it happens.

  5. Starting ten years earlier usually beats doubling the amount. Time is the input that compounds; money is not.

  7. The same machinery, pointed at you

    A credit card runs on exactly this. Unpaid interest joins the balance and earns interest of its own, which is why minimum payments can take decades and why clearing expensive debt beats saving.

  8. Zoom out

    Compounding is not a financial trick. It is what any process that feeds on its own output does, and it is equally indifferent to which direction it is pointed.
