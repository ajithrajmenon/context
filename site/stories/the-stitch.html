Why do you get a stitch when you run?

A stitch is one of the most common experiences in sport and one of the least explained. The confident answer you were given at school has not survived testing.

3 min read · you will come away knowing what actually prevents it, and why the folk explanation survived being wrong.

  1. The diaphragm story is almost certainly wrong

    The standard explanation is that the diaphragm cramps because it is short of blood. It is tidy, it is decades old, and it fails on the evidence: stitches happen in swimming and horse riding, where the breathing pattern is nothing like running, and they happen in people whose diaphragm is working perfectly.

  2. Everyone has had one. Nobody is certain what it is.

  4. The best current answer is friction, not cramp

    Two smooth layers line your abdominal cavity and normally slide over each other. Fill the stomach, or move hard for long enough, and that sliding becomes rubbing. The tissue is richly supplied with nerves, which is why the pain is sharp, local, and pointable-at in a way that muscle cramp never is.

  5. It is probably not your diaphragm at all. It is two sheets of tissue inside your abdomen rubbing where they should be gliding.

  7. Why the wrong answer lasted

    The cramp explanation persisted because it is easy to say, hard to test, and nobody dies of a stitch. It is a good demonstration that an explanation being widespread is not evidence for it, only evidence that it is memorable.

  8. Zoom out

    There is a whole layer of everyday medicine like this: universal, harmless, and genuinely unresolved. Common does not mean understood.
