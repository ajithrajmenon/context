Death

What actually happens when you die?

We talk about death as a moment: a line crossed, a light going out. Biologically there is no line. There is a sequence, it runs in a fixed order, and it takes much longer than almost anyone expects.

5 min read · you will come away knowing what the order of shutdown means for donation, and for how we define death.

  1. The heart stops. Almost nothing else does.

    Your brain has roughly ten seconds of oxygen left in it once circulation ends. That part is quick, and it is the part everyone pictures.

    Everything else carries on. Your cells have no way of knowing anything has happened. They have fuel and a job, and they keep doing it for minutes to hours.

  3. Your cells never get the message

    They have fuel and a job, and no way of knowing that the body they belong to has stopped. Most of them carry on for hours.

  4. You do not die all at once. Different parts of you stop at different times, over hours.

  5. Which is why transplant medicine exists

    A donated heart can be restarted in someone else hours after its owner died. A cornea works after a week or more.

    If death were a single instant, none of that would be possible. The whole field lives inside the gap between the first thing stopping and the last.

  7. So the definition had to be rewritten

    For thousands of years, death meant the heart had stopped. Then we learned to restart hearts, and people came back.

    The line moved to the brain — the one part that cannot be restarted, replaced or kept going by a machine.

  10. Zoom out

    Nothing about you is destroyed. Every atom carries on and ends up somewhere else. What ends is the arrangement — and the arrangement was always the temporary part. Knowing the order it comes apart in is also, very practically, why one death can leave several people alive.
