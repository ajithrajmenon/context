What if the Earth stopped spinning?

You are being carried eastward right now at close to the speed of a rifle bullet, and you cannot detect it, because everything around you is moving too.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why the spin you cannot feel is holding the whole system together.

  1. Motion you share is motion you cannot feel

    At the equator the surface travels about 1,670 km/h. You do not feel it for the same reason you do not feel 900 km/h in a cruising aircraft: nothing near you is moving differently. What you feel is not speed but change of speed.

  2. You would keep going. So would the air, at about 1,600 km/h.

  4. Stopping is the change, and the change is everything

    If the surface stopped and you did not, you would continue eastward at your local speed. So would the oceans and, worst of all, the atmosphere — a global wind at highway-to-supersonic speed, scouring everything not sheltered by terrain. That is the instantaneous version, and it is the one that makes the good picture.

  5. The spin is not something the Earth does to you. It is something you are doing with the Earth, which is exactly why stopping it would be catastrophic and continuing it is imperceptible.

  7. The slow version is real

    The Earth genuinely is slowing, by tides dragging against the ocean floor — about 1.8 milliseconds per century. Days were roughly 22 hours when the dinosaurs were around, and coral growth bands preserve the record. It is not going to stop. It is a useful reminder that the planet's settings are not constants; they are just changing far more slowly than we do.

  8. Zoom out

    The most consequential thing about your planet is a motion you have never once felt, and the reason you have never felt it is the reason it would kill you to lose it.
