What if every insect disappeared?

The thought experiment is usually run on bees and pollination. That is the smaller half of the problem, and not the half that arrives first.

3 min read · you will come away knowing which insects actually matter, and what a garden can do about it.

  1. Pollination is the part everyone names

    About three quarters of leading food crops benefit from animal pollination, and roughly a third of global food volume comes from crops that depend on it to some degree. Lose that and you lose most fruit, most nuts, coffee, cocoa and a great many vegetables. Staple grains — wheat, rice, maize — are wind-pollinated and would survive.

  2. Not a slow decline. A food system that stops inside one growing season.

  4. The clean-up crew is the load-bearing wall

    Insects are the primary means by which dead material re-enters the soil. Dung beetles alone save cattle industries billions a year by clearing pasture; without them, grazing land fouls and goes out of use within a season. Leaf litter stops breaking down, nutrients stop cycling, and the base of the soil food web fails. Above that, roughly 60% of bird species eat insects, and freshwater fish depend on insect larvae almost entirely.

  5. The collapse would not come through the fruit aisle. It would come through the soil, because the things that clear the dead are the things nobody counts.

  7. It is already happening, slowly

    Long-running monitoring finds substantial declines in insect biomass in several well-studied regions — the most cited found around 75% over 27 years in German protected areas. That figure is contested and the global picture is uneven, with some groups stable and freshwater insects in places recovering. What is not contested is that the trend is downward where farming is intensive, and that habitat loss and pesticide use are the main drivers.

  8. Zoom out

    This is the rare thought experiment with a dial rather than a switch. We are not going to lose every insect. We are turning the dial, and the part that would hurt most is the part we notice least.
