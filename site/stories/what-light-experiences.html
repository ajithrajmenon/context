Light

For light, no time passes at all

Go faster and your clock slows down. Everyone knows that bit. What is less well known is what happens when you reach the top speed — because the clock does not slow. It stops.

  1. Speed borrows from time

    Everything is always moving through space and through time together, and there is a fixed budget shared between them.

    Stand still and all of it goes into time. Move fast and you spend some of it on space, so less is left for the clock.

  2. Light spends the whole budget on motion

    Nothing is left over. Its clock does not run slowly — it does not run.

  3. A photon that crossed the entire universe was emitted and absorbed in the same instant, as far as it is concerned.

  5. This is why the speed limit exists

    It is not that we lack a fast enough engine. At light speed there is no time left to experience, so there is nothing further to go.

    The limit is not a wall in front of you. It is the edge of what moving even means.

  6. Every star you see reached you instantly

    From your side, that light is thousands of years old. From its side, it left and arrived in the same moment. Both are completely true.

  7. Zoom out

    Time is not a stage everything sits on. It is something each thing has its own supply of, spent at its own rate — and light, moving as fast as anything can, has none to spend at all.
