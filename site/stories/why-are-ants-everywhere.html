Why are ants everywhere?

Ants have been running the same business model for a hundred million years, on every continent except Antarctica, and no single ant has any idea how any of it works.

  1. The numbers are hard to hold

    The best estimate is about twenty thousand trillion ants alive right now. For every person, there are roughly two and a half million of them.

    Weighed together they come to something like all the wild birds and wild mammals on Earth, combined.

  2. A colony behaves like one animal

    Trails form, jobs get allocated, the nest gets repaired. Nobody is directing any of it.

  3. The queen is not in charge of anything

    She lays eggs. That is the whole job. She issues no orders and has no overview of the colony.

    Every decision comes from individual ants following simple local rules — mostly "follow the strongest smell" and "do the job nobody else is doing".

  4. No ant knows the plan. There is no plan. The colony still farms, builds, wages war and adapts.

  6. They invented farming first

    Some ants have been growing fungus underground for fifty million years, weeding it, fertilising it and dosing it with antibiotics they carry on their bodies.

  7. Why this keeps coming up elsewhere

    Simple parts, local rules, no central control, complicated behaviour out the far end. That is ants, and it is also your brain cells, and flocks, and markets.

    The lesson ants keep teaching is that you do not need anyone in charge to get something that looks designed.

  8. Zoom out

    One ant is close to nothing — a few hundred thousand neurons and a handful of reflexes. A million of them, following the same reflexes, build something that farms. Nothing was added but number.
