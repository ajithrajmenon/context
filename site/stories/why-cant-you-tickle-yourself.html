Why can you not tickle yourself?

Anyone can tickle you. You cannot. The reason is one of the most useful facts in neuroscience, and it explains far more than tickling.

3 min read · you will come away knowing what the same prediction machinery explains about hearing voices.

  1. It is not about surprise

    The obvious answer is that you know it is coming. But you can know exactly when someone else will tickle you and still be tickled. Prediction of timing is not what is doing the work.

  2. Your brain predicts your own movements and cancels the sensation before it arrives.

  4. The forward model

    Every time you move, your brain sends a copy of the command to a predictor, which forecasts the sensory consequence and subtracts it. This is why the world does not appear to lurch when you move your eyes.

  5. You cannot tickle yourself because your brain already knew, in detail, exactly what it was about to feel.

  7. Why this matters well beyond tickling

    If the predictor misfires, self-generated actions stop feeling self-generated. This is a leading account of why some people experience their own inner speech as an external voice — the same machinery, failing quietly.

  8. Zoom out

    A party trick and a serious psychiatric hypothesis turn out to be the same mechanism, examined at two different volumes.
