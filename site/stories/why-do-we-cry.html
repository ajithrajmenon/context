Emotion

Why do we cry?

You produce tears constantly and never notice. The ones you notice are chemically distinct, and appear to be aimed at other people.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why crying alone feels nothing like crying with someone.

  1. Not one fluid, three

    Basal tears coat the eye every second of the day. Reflex tears flood it when something irritates. Emotional tears are the third kind, and analysis finds a different composition — more protein, and hormones absent from the other two.

  2. Three different kinds of tears, made of different things, doing different jobs.

  4. The strange part is who it is for

    Crying blurs your vision at the exact moment you most need to read a situation. That is a cost, and evolution does not usually pay costs for nothing. The leading explanation is that the cost is the point: visible tears are a hard-to-fake signal of distress, and they reliably pull help out of other people.

  5. Emotional tears are not a release valve. They are a message, and the recipient is somebody else.

  7. Which explains the loneliness of it

    People consistently report that crying alone helps far less than crying with someone who responds well. If tears are a signal, a signal with no receiver has done nothing. The relief people describe comes mostly from the response, not the fluid.

  8. Zoom out

    It is a strange thing to discover about yourself: that one of your most private experiences is built to be seen.
