Why do we get bored?

Boredom is genuinely unpleasant — people will give themselves small electric shocks rather than sit alone with nothing to do. That is a strange amount of pressure for a feeling that seems to do nothing.

  1. It is an alarm, not a mood

    Bored means two things at once: you want to be engaged with something, and nothing available is doing it.

    That gap is the whole feeling. It is your attention system reporting that it is being wasted.

  2. Attention will not sit still for something worthless

    The focus keeps sliding off, and everything at the edges starts looking more interesting than the middle.

  3. And that is exactly the point

    An animal that felt fine doing nothing forever would sit in one place and starve. Boredom is the push that makes you go and look.

    People made to do something tedious then score higher on tests of coming up with new ideas. The restlessness is doing something.

  4. Boredom is not the absence of something to do. It is your brain telling you that what you are doing is not worth it.

  6. Why a phone makes it worse

    An endless feed is engaging enough to stop the discomfort and never engaging enough to satisfy it. The alarm gets muffled instead of answered.

  7. Being bored on purpose

    Ideas turn up in showers and on walks because those are the last places left with nothing to look at.

    If you never let yourself be bored, you never get the part that comes after it.

  8. Zoom out

    Boredom is a demand for something better, and it is one of the few feelings that goes away only when you actually do something worth doing.
