Why do we get bored?

Boredom is unpleasant, universal, and does something. It is not a gap in experience; it is a verdict.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why the fix is a harder task, not more entertainment.

  1. It is not about having nothing to do

    People report boredom while scrolling through unlimited content, in busy jobs, in the middle of a film. And people report deep absorption while doing something objectively repetitive. The trigger is not the quantity of stimulus.

  2. Not the absence of something to do. A signal that what you are doing is not worth your attention.

  4. It is an alarm about attention

    The best current account treats boredom as a signal that your attention is being spent on something that is not paying. It is aversive on purpose, because a mild signal you could ignore would not move you. Given the discomfort, people will do genuinely unpleasant things to escape it — in one well-known study, participants left alone with their thoughts chose to give themselves electric shocks rather than sit there.

  5. Boredom is not emptiness. It is your attention system filing a complaint, and it is designed to be unbearable enough that you act on it.

  7. The part worth keeping

    Boredom reliably precedes mind-wandering, and mind-wandering is where a good deal of planning, problem-solving and creative recombination happens. Children left genuinely unoccupied invent things. An alarm that is never allowed to ring is an alarm you have disconnected.

  8. Zoom out

    The uncomfortable feeling is the useful part. We have built a world extremely good at silencing it and are only starting to notice what that cost.
