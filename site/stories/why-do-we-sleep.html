Sleep

Why do we sleep?

Lying still and unaware for eight hours is close to the most dangerous thing an animal can do. Every animal with a brain does it anyway. Whatever sleep buys, it is worth being eaten over.

5 min read · you will come away knowing why you feel fine on six hours and are not fine on six hours.

  1. It is far too expensive to be an accident

    While asleep you cannot eat, defend yourself or watch for anything. If sleep were optional, some animal would have dropped it and out-competed the rest.

    None has. Not one. Whatever is happening up there, it cannot be done while the lights are on.

  3. The brain is rinsed while you are out

    During deep sleep the gaps between brain cells widen and fluid is pushed through, carrying out waste built up during the day.

    It works far better asleep than awake — a good reason for the brain to do it while you are not using it.

  5. Nothing about the brain is idle at night

    It is running a maintenance schedule it cannot run while you are using it — which is a very different thing from being switched off.

  6. Sleep is not the brain switching off. It is maintenance that cannot run while you are using the machine.

  7. And here is the part that catches people

    Lose sleep steadily and your performance drops and stays down. Your sense of how you are doing recovers within a couple of days.

    So people running on five hours reliably report feeling fine. Measured, they are not fine. The gauge is broken, not the engine.

  10. What actually works

    1. 1

      A fixed wake time

      More useful than a fixed bedtime. The body clock anchors to when light arrives, not when you switch off.

    2. 2

      Light in the morning

      Ten minutes outdoors shortly after waking does more for the clock than anything you do at night.

    3. 3

      Stop counting the good nights

      One long lie-in does not clear a week of debt. Consistency beats catching up.

    4. 4

      Treat caffeine as long-acting

      Half of a 3pm coffee is still in you at 9pm, quietly shortening the deep half of the night.

  11. Zoom out

    You will spend around twenty-six years of your life asleep. Not lost time — it is the maintenance window that makes the other fifty-odd years work at all. It is also the one health decision available to everybody, for free, tonight.
