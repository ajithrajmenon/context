Why does a healing cut itch?

A wound that has stopped hurting starts itching, and the urge to scratch it is close to irresistible. Both of those facts have specific explanations.

3 min read · you will come away knowing why scratching feels so good and makes it worse, and what breaks the loop.

  1. Itch is not mild pain

    For a long time itch was assumed to be pain turned down. It is not. It runs on its own dedicated fibres with their own signalling molecules.

    The clearest evidence is that opioids relieve pain and often make itch worse. Two systems that behaved the same way would not do that.

  2. Itch has its own nerve pathway. It is not weak pain, and scratching hijacks it.

  4. Healing itself produces the itch

    As a wound closes, new nerve endings grow into the repairing tissue and are mechanically stretched by the contracting scar. Histamine released during repair adds to it.

    So the itch is a by-product of the repair working, arriving exactly when the danger has passed.

  5. Scratching does not relieve the itch. It replaces it with mild pain, which the brain prefers, and the itch returns worse.

  7. What to do instead

    Cold works, because cold fibres inhibit itch fibres directly. So does firm pressure, for the same reason.

    Keeping the area moisturised reduces mechanical stretch on the new nerve endings. Antihistamines help histamine itch and are close to useless for the rest.

  8. Zoom out

    An itch you are told to ignore is not weakness of will. It is a dedicated alarm system doing its job at exactly the moment its job has become pointless.
