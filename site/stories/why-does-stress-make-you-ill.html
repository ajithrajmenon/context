Your body

Why does stress make you ill?

The system that saves you from a genuine threat is the same one that runs all week over an email. It does not distinguish between them, and it was never built to stay on.

3 min read · you will come away knowing which symptoms are the response rather than the problem, and what shortens it.

  1. It is a brilliant emergency system

    Within seconds of a threat your heart rate climbs, glucose floods the blood, and non-urgent work — digestion, repair, reproduction, immune housekeeping — is postponed.

    For a genuine emergency lasting minutes, this is close to ideal. Nothing about it is a design flaw.

  2. The stress response is superb for an emergency and corrosive when it never switches off.

  4. The damage is in what gets postponed

    Repair, immune surveillance and digestion are not cancelled, they are deferred. Deferred indefinitely, the deferral becomes the illness.

    This is why chronic stress shows up as poor sleep, frequent infections, gut trouble and slow healing rather than as one named disease.

  5. Nothing about the stress response is broken. It is a short-term system being asked to run permanently, and that is a different thing.

  7. What actually turns it off

    The response ends when the body gets evidence the emergency is over. Physical exertion, slow exhalation and social contact all supply that evidence directly.

    Understanding this is why exercise helps a problem it cannot solve: it completes the cycle the body started and never finished.

  8. Zoom out

    Stress is not an enemy to be eliminated. It is an unfinished sentence, and the body is waiting for the full stop.
