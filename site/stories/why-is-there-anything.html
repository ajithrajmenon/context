Matter

Why is there something instead of nothing?

Take a box. Remove all the air, all the light, all the heat, every last particle. What is left is still not nothing — and that turns out to be one of the deepest facts we know.

  1. Perfect emptiness is forbidden

    To be truly empty, a patch of space would have to hold exactly zero energy and stay exactly that way.

    The rules of the very small do not permit anything to be that precisely pinned down. So space fidgets.

  2. Pairs appear and cancel out constantly

    A particle and its opposite, borrowed from nothing, gone again before the universe notices the debt. Everywhere. Always.

  3. We can measure it

    Put two metal plates extremely close together in a vacuum and they get pushed together by the fidgeting outside them.

    That push has been measured. Empty space is doing something, and it is strong enough to move objects.

  4. Nothing may simply be an unstable state. Given the rules we have, something is what you get by default.

  6. It does not finish the job

    This tells us why there is not nothing. It still does not tell us where the rules came from. That question is entirely open, and may stay that way.

  7. Zoom out

    We have moved the mystery rather than solved it. But moving it was real progress: "why is there anything" turned out to have a physical answer, and the leftover question is now sharper than the one we started with.
