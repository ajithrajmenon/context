Why is the wifi bad in that one room?

A room fifteen feet away can be unusable while one forty feet away down a hallway is fine. Distance is not what is doing this.

3 min read · you will come away knowing the free change that fixes most dead rooms.

  1. Distance is the wrong variable

    Open air barely troubles a wifi signal. A solid wall takes a large bite out of it, and brick, concrete, tile and anything containing water or metal are far worse than a plasterboard partition.

  2. Count walls, not metres. Where the router sits matters more than what it cost.

  4. The corner problem

    A router radiates roughly all around itself. Put it in the corner of a flat and a large share of its output goes into the street or next door. Move it to the middle and the same hardware covers far more of your home.

  5. Where the router sits is worth more than what the router cost.

  7. Before you buy anything

    Move the router to the middle, out in the open, off the floor. Keep it away from the microwave and the fish tank. If one room is still dead, a mesh point or a wired access point beats a bigger router every time.

  8. Zoom out

    Almost every dead room is a placement problem wearing the costume of a hardware problem.
