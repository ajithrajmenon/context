Why do you yawn, and why is it catching?

You yawned before you were born. Foetuses yawn in the womb, where breathing has not started and there is no air to be short of.

3 min read · you will come away knowing what catching a yawn says about how close you are to someone.

  1. The oxygen explanation is dead

    The story is that yawning tops up oxygen. It was tested directly: give people air with more oxygen or more carbon dioxide and yawning does not change. Breathing rate changes, as you would expect. Yawning does not.

  2. Not oxygen. That explanation was tested and failed decades ago.

  4. The best explanation is temperature

    A yawn draws a large volume of cool air past a rich blood supply and stretches the jaw, which increases blood flow to the head. Brain temperature rises slightly before a yawn and falls after. Yawning also falls away when the surrounding air is warmer than the body, which is exactly what a cooling mechanism should do and nothing an oxygen mechanism would predict.

  5. A yawn appears to be a radiator, not a breath. It is your brain shedding a little heat at a moment of transition.

  7. And the catching part is social

    Contagious yawning is a separate phenomenon riding on the first. It appears around the age of four or five, the same window in which children start reliably reading other people's mental states, and its strength follows social closeness. It is not a reflex. It is a low-level measure of how much attention your brain gives to that particular person.

  8. Zoom out

    Two different systems are wearing the same behaviour: one regulating your head, the other quietly reporting who you are bonded to.
