Money

How does a little money turn into a lot?

Short answer

Money you put away earns a little. Then that little earns too. For years it looks like nothing is happening — the gap only opens up late, and then it opens fast.

Why it looks like nothing for so long

In year one, your savings earn a small amount. In year two, your savings and that small amount both earn. The extra bit is tiny, so early on the whole thing looks like a slightly better piggy bank.

But the growing part keeps growing, and it grows on itself. Halfway along, the interest starts earning more each year than you are putting in, and the line bends away from the dashed one.

This is also how debt works

Exactly the same machinery runs behind a credit card, only pointed at you. Unpaid interest joins the balance and then earns interest of its own.

That is why a card balance you only make minimum payments on can take decades to clear, and why clearing expensive debt beats saving almost every time.

What matters most

  • Starting earlier beats saving more. Ten extra years usually outweighs doubling the amount.
  • Make it automatic on payday so it never becomes a decision.
  • Pay off anything charging more than your savings earn, first.
