Why is the fridge the worst place for bread?

Short answer

Going stale is starch quietly recrystallising, and that happens fastest just above freezing. The fridge sits exactly in the worst zone. The counter is slower; the freezer nearly stops it.

Stale is not dry

It is easy to assume bread goes hard because it dries out. Wrap a loaf in a sealed bag so it cannot lose any water and it still goes stale.

What is happening is that starch, forced into a loose disordered arrangement by baking, slowly settles back into ordered crystals. Those crystals grab water and stiffen the crumb. The bread is not drier — it is firmer, and holding its water differently.

Why the fridge is the trap

That recrystallising happens fastest a little above freezing — right where your fridge lives. Chilling bread makes it stale faster than leaving it out.

Below freezing the molecules barely move, so a freezer nearly stops the clock. This is also why warming a stale slice revives it: heat breaks the crystals up again, for a while.

Where to actually put it

  • Eating it in a couple of days: counter, in a paper bag or a bread bin.
  • Longer than that: slice it and freeze it, then toast straight from frozen.
  • The fridge is for sandwiches, not for loaves.
  • A stale slice in a warm oven for a few minutes comes back to life.
