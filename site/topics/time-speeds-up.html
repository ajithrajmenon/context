Your head

Why does a year feel faster every time?

Short answer

Your brain judges a year against everything you have already lived. At eight, one year is an eighth of your whole life. At forty it is a fortieth — the same year, a far smaller slice.

Nothing is actually speeding up

A year is the same length it has always been. What changes is what you are comparing it against.

When you are eight, twelve months is a huge fraction of everything you can remember. At forty, the same twelve months lands on top of a much bigger pile, so it takes up less room.

The other half is novelty

Your memory stores new things and skips repeats. A first summer at a new school is dense with firsts, so it leaves a thick trace and feels long when you look back.

A year of the same commute compresses into almost nothing, because there was little worth filing separately.

What actually slows it down

  • Do things you have never done before — new places make thick memories.
  • Break routine deliberately, even in small ways: a different route, a different room.
  • Write things down. A year you can recall in detail is a year that felt long.
